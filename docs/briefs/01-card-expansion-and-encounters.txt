MECHANICA DESCENT

Card Expansion & Encounter Implementation Brief

Scope: 12 base cards, 7 named variants/upgrades, new encounter behaviors, branching choices, special interactions, and supporting systems.

Status: PROPOSED — implementation specification, not confirmation of existing functionality.

⸻

1. Core implementation goals

Expand Mechanica Descent with a connected collection of mechanical allies, salvage parasites, improvised equipment, hazardous technology, and anomalous facility encounters.

The three principal additions are:

1. Half-Awake Builder Drone: An autonomous machine ally with imperfect but useful maintenance behavior, upgrade paths, and special encounters.
2. Salvage Leech: A reusable parasite that exploits damaged machines, creates economic opportunities, and provokes enemy countermeasures.
3. Wrong Hallway: A rare encounter modifier producing additional events, navigation decisions, risks, and salvage opportunities.

Also implement the other nine cards previously proposed, including their distinctive behaviors and synergies.

Design requirements

* Preserve established combat, deck, navigation, Energy, Scrap, Madness, Threat, and damage mechanics wherever present.
* Integrate with existing enemy classes rather than replacing enemy AI.
* Ensure every card produces visible gameplay feedback.
* Provide meaningful choices around additional danger versus additional rewards.
* Make friendly machines and enemies respond to attachments, repairs, salvage, and altered navigation.
* Allow machines to malfunction without becoming randomly useless.
* Avoid permanent softlocks, unlimited resource generation, and unstoppable card-effect recursion.
* Keep new probabilities and costs configurable for balancing.
* Do not assume that an unverified mechanic is already implemented.

Where this brief uses a combat system concept not currently supported, implement the smallest compatible equivalent rather than creating an unrelated subsystem.

⸻

2. Common card and encounter framework

2.1 Card categories

Support these tags for the new content:

Category	Function
Machine Ally	Deploys an allied machine with HP and autonomous behaviors
Attachment	Connects a persistent effect to a target
Equipment	Modifies an existing character or weapon
Consumable	Produces an immediate or limited-charge effect
Structure	Deploys a persistent defensive or tactical object
Reaction	Activates in response to a combat event
Encounter Modifier	Alters a room’s progression, rewards, or threats
Nightmare	Interacts with Madness or anomalous behavior

Cards can carry multiple tags.

2.2 Proposed timing rules

Use a consistent event sequence:

1. Player chooses an action.
2. Validate cost, target, and available actions.
3. Pay costs.
4. Resolve attacks, movement, or direct effects.
5. Process damage and associated triggers.
6. Process destruction and death effects.
7. Resolve attachments and salvage recovery.
8. Resolve end-of-turn machine behaviors.
9. Check combat completion and encounter modifiers.
10. Present rewards or navigation choices.

Effects must not recursively trigger themselves unless explicitly permitted.

In particular:

* Leech damage cannot trigger another Leech extraction.
* Builder Drone repair does not count as salvage damage.
* Gun Nest attacks cannot generate additional Gun Nest actions.
* Dead Pilot’s Reflex cannot generate a second Reflex through its own effects.
* Wrong Hallway cannot trigger another Wrong Hallway inside its own extension encounter.

2.3 Persistence

Track each deployed machine’s:

* HP and maximum HP.
* Current room and position, if positioning exists.
* Active attachment or upgrade.
* Behavior state and cooldown.
* Remaining charges, where applicable.
* Ownership and faction.
* Temporary disabling effects.
* Pending salvage rewards.
* Whether it has acted this turn.

Use the game’s existing rules for ally persistence between rooms. If allies do not persist, preserve only card ownership and upgrades.

All random rolls must use the run’s seeded RNG when available, so identical actions under identical states reproduce identical results.

3. Half-Awake Builder Drone

Type: Machine Ally
Rarity: Common
Proposed cost: 2 Scrap
Stats: 4 HP / 1 damage

Primary effect — Uncertain Maintenance

Every second turn after deployment, the drone performs one maintenance operation.

Patch Job

* Restore 2 HP to a damaged allied Tech card or compatible mechanical object.
* Cannot exceed target maximum HP.
* Prioritize eligible targets with the largest missing HP percentage.
* Resolve ties randomly using seeded RNG.

Scrap Sorting

* Generate 1 Scrap.
* Show an animation of the drone dragging, sorting, or dismantling mechanical debris.

Selection rules

* If only repair is possible, repair.
* If only salvage is possible, salvage.
* If both are possible, choose randomly.
* If neither is possible, enter a short idle animation without consuming the next maintenance opportunity.

The drone’s Scrap generation represents small recoverable machine fragments, not duplication of existing high-value loot.

Secondary ability — Emergency Repair

The player may spend 1 Energy to command an immediate 1 HP repair.

* Requires a valid damaged allied mechanical target.
* Can be used once per player turn.
* Does not reset the normal two-turn maintenance cycle.
* Cannot repair the drone itself unless permitted by existing self-repair rules.

Wreckage response — Builder’s Instinct

When another allied machine is destroyed, the Builder Drone remembers its wreckage.

On its next autonomous action, it attempts to move toward that wreckage if movement is supported.

Once near the wreckage, it may:

* Collect ordinary salvage.
* Perform a permitted recovery interaction.
* Resume its normal maintenance work.

This behavior must never override a direct player command.

Last Useful Act

When destroyed, the drone leaves 1 recoverable Scrap.

The Scrap is awarded when the wreckage is safely recovered, or at room completion if the game has no spatial salvage collection.

Do not generate Scrap repeatedly from revival or redeployment of the same destroyed instance.

3.1 Builder Drone upgrade paths

Implement the following as mutually exclusive upgrades to the base card. A run may contain multiple Builder Drones with different upgrades, but one drone instance may use only one specialization.

A. Fully Awake Drone

* Player chooses Patch Job or Scrap Sorting whenever a maintenance action is available.
* Keeps the same two-turn interval.
* Keeps the 2 HP repair and 1 Scrap generation values.
* Retains Emergency Repair.

This is the dependable control-focused upgrade.

B. Scavenger Drone

* Generates 2 Scrap per maintenance activation.
* Cannot perform Patch Job.
* Can still move toward wreckage and collect salvage.
* Emergency Repair is disabled.

This version rewards salvage-heavy decks.

C. Overworked Drone

* Performs one maintenance action every turn.
* After every third successful maintenance action, loses 1 HP.
* Can be repaired by another machine.
* Must give the player a visible warning when nearing destruction.

This version provides rapid value but is difficult to sustain.

D. Possessed Builder

* Repairs 3 HP per activation.
* When damaged allied and enemy Tech targets are both eligible, has a proposed 20% chance of selecting an enemy target.
* Otherwise prefers allied machinery.
* Has unsettling animation and audio cues.
* Does not automatically switch factions.

The malfunction chance and healing amount are TUNE LATER values.

3.2 Autonomous behavior and personality

Give builder drones compact but distinct animations:

* Repeatedly welding the same piece of armor.
* Dragging oversized metal panels.
* Extending several small repair arms.
* Sorting scrap into neat piles.
* Becoming confused between two damaged machines.
* Attempting to repair a destroyed ally before recognizing its condition.
* Emitting a distressed chirp when machinery collapses nearby.
* Performing a brief celebration after a successful repair.

Animations should be functional and inexpensive to produce.

3.3 Special event — The Drone Remembers

A living allied Builder Drone may recognize an abandoned maintenance signal while exploring eligible facility rooms.

The drone attempts to reach a sealed wall, machinery hatch, or unfinished construction project.

Present three options:

Follow the Drone

* Enter a short optional exploration event.
* Potential results: hidden salvage, machine parts, an unfinished device, or a dangerous reactivated system.
* The drone remains exposed to event hazards.

Recall the Drone

* Cancel exploration.
* Preserve the ally.
* Lose the optional special reward.

Allow Independent Investigation

* The drone temporarily becomes unavailable.
* After a short interval, it returns with salvage, returns damaged, or requires recovery.
* Never remove the drone permanently without a clearly communicated and avoidable risk.

Avoid frequent repetition of this event during one run.

4. Salvage Leech

Type: Tech Attachment
Rarity: Uncommon
Cost: 1 Scrap
Target: Enemy machine or compatible mechanical enemy

Primary effect — Opportunistic Extraction

Attach the Leech to an eligible enemy.

Whenever its host receives at least 3 total damage during one turn:

* Generate 1 Scrap.
* Play a mechanical cutting or extraction effect.
* Advance its extraction counter.

Maximum: 3 successful extractions per attachment.

The trigger checks cumulative qualifying damage during the turn, not every individual hit.

Armor Stripping

After the second successful extraction, reduce the host’s armor by 1 for the remainder of the encounter.

Apply this once per Leech attachment.

The Leech cannot repeatedly strip the same armor point by detaching and reattaching during the same encounter.

Host destruction

When the host dies:

* Resolve final qualifying damage and extraction first.
* Roll a proposed 25% destruction chance.
* On survival, return the Leech card to the discard pile.
* On destruction, remove that deployed Leech instance from the current encounter.

Permanent removal from the player’s deck is not part of the default implementation.

Permanent loss may be considered separately as a Nightmare difficulty modifier.

Alternative action — Risky Extraction

The player may manually cash out an attached Leech.

* Gain 2 Scrap.
* Deal 1 damage to the host.
* Destroy the deployed Leech instance for the encounter.
* This effect cannot trigger its own extraction.
* Only available once per attachment.

If this action kills the host, resolve normal enemy death and rewards without adding another Leech extraction.

4.1 Enemy reactions

Give enemies different responses based on behavioral tags.

Defensive machine

* Attempts to shake off or remove the Leech.
* May sacrifice its own attack to perform a purge.
* Prioritizes protecting damaged components.

Aggressive machine

* Continues attacking.
* May become more reckless.
* Can accidentally expose additional weak points.
* Does not automatically lose offensive capability.

Repair-capable enemy

* May target the Leech with a removal operation.
* May abandon repairs to perform the operation.
* Other allied machines may assist.

Piloted enemy machine

* Displays alarmed radio dialogue.
* May attempt an emergency system purge.
* May tear away one armor point to remove the parasite.

Example dialogue:

* “Something’s cutting into my chassis!”
* “Maintenance, get this thing OFF!”
* “It’s inside the armor plating!”
* “Forget the damage! Pull it out!”

Heavy enemy or boss

May possess a purge action that ejects the Leech onto the battlefield.

An ejected Leech remains recoverable for one player turn.

Possible player responses:

* Retrieve it.
* Allow a Builder Drone to collect it.
* Reattach it to a compatible enemy if normal action rules permit.
* Abandon it.

If an enemy crushes the ejected Leech, it is lost for that encounter.

All AI counters need visible preparation or telegraphing.

5. Salvage Leech variants

5.1 Blood-Scrap Leech

Type: Tech Attachment / Nightmare

Whenever extraction succeeds:

* Generate the normal Scrap.
* Deal 1 additional damage to the host.
* The additional damage cannot trigger extraction.

Retains the normal attachment limit.

This is a more aggressive version of the Salvage Leech.

5.2 Symbiotic Leech

Type: Allied Tech Attachment

Attach to a friendly machine.

Whenever that machine receives a successful repair:

* Generate 1 Scrap.
* Maximum twice per encounter.

Self-healing, repeated zero-value repair attempts, and blocked overhealing must not activate it.

A repair must restore at least 1 actual HP.

This creates synergy with Builder Drones and defensive structures.

5.3 Queen Salvager

Type: Rare Tech Attachment

Receives the base Leech extraction effects.

When its host dies, it may deploy one temporary basic Salvage Leech onto another eligible surviving enemy.

Restrictions:

* Maximum one offspring per Queen Salvager attachment.
* Offspring cannot create additional offspring.
* Offspring expires after the current encounter.
* Requires a valid enemy target.
* Cannot attach to an already occupied incompatible attachment slot.
* If no target exists, the reproduction effect is skipped.

The Queen itself still resolves its normal survival or destruction roll.

6. Wrong Hallway

Type: Encounter Modifier
Rarity: Rare
Theme: Facility anomaly / spatial inconsistency

Basic trigger

When activated on an eligible room, Wrong Hallway modifies its completion sequence.

The normal encounter occurs first.

After it finishes, the expected exit disappears or no longer matches the room layout.

The player receives a navigation decision rather than immediately completing the room.

An additional encounter, obstacle, or choice-based event may follow.

Hard limits

* Only one Wrong Hallway extension per normal room.
* Wrong Hallway cannot trigger itself.
* Extra enemies cannot generate unlimited duplicate loot.
* The player must always have a resolvable escape route.
* Failed optional exploration must not erase previously earned base-room rewards.
* Navigation must eventually return to the normal progression system.

6.1 Player navigation choices

Option A — Force the Exit

Cost: 2 Energy.

The player forces a mechanical exit, cuts through a malfunctioning lock, or overrides the facility system.

Effects:

* Avoid the additional encounter.
* Claim only 1 bonus Scrap instead of the full anomaly reward.
* Return to ordinary room progression.

If insufficient Energy exists, disable the option visibly. Do not make it the only escape method.

Option B — Search the Walls

Spend one exploration action investigating unusual panels, door seams, or machinery.

Proposed outcomes:

* 60%: Find a small cache worth 2 Scrap.
* 40%: Trigger a minor hazard or alert an enemy.

A successful search does not automatically end the hallway.

Prevent repeated farming by permitting only one wall search per anomaly.

Option C — Follow the Noise

Commit immediately to the hallway’s additional encounter.

* Reveal the selected event.
* Do not charge the normal Force the Exit Energy cost.
* Permit event-specific rewards and interactions.
* In exchange, expose the player to its full danger.

Option D — Send a Builder Drone

Available when an allied Builder Drone is active.

Temporarily dispatch the drone into an alternate route.

Potential results:

* Discover a safe passage.
* Find extra Scrap.
* Reveal the next enemy.
* Become separated.
* Return damaged.
* Activate a hidden maintenance system.

Proposed default outcome weights:

* 40% safe reconnaissance.
* 25% salvage discovery.
* 20% separated drone.
* 15% hazardous discovery.

These probabilities are TUNE LATER.

The drone must remain recoverable if separated.

Option E — Mark the Corridor

Record the unusual geometry using navigation equipment or a manual marking.

Effects:

* Gain a temporary navigation knowledge flag.
* On the next Wrong Hallway occurrence, reveal one extra piece of information before choosing.
* Potentially reduce the next anomaly’s initial danger.
* Consume the flag after use.

This must not create an unrestricted stack of permanent navigation benefits.

7. Wrong Hallway event pool

Implement all four primary events below, plus the special combined-card event in Section 8.

7.1 Maintenance Shift

The player enters a room full of apparently dormant construction machinery.

Multiple Builder Drones activate and attempt to repair surrounding objects.

Initial state

* Machines are neutral.
* Some repair damaged structures.
* Some attempt to repair disabled hostile machines.
* Others are occupied with harmless tasks.

Player options

Remain Unnoticed

* Move carefully through the maintenance area.
* Avoid hostile interaction if no alarms are triggered.
* Receive a small safe salvage reward.

Redirect the Drones

* Spend an available interaction or compatible resource.
* Attempt to redirect repairs toward the player’s equipment.
* Success grants repair value.
* Failure may activate nearby machinery.

Dismantle the Drones

* Attack neutral units for Scrap.
* Nearby maintenance machines become defensive or hostile.
* Risk losing valuable environmental assistance.

Assist Maintenance

* Spend Scrap or Energy to complete a repair.
* Unlock a larger cache or possible machine ally reward.

Behavior requirements

Neutral drones must visibly respond to:

* Damage.
* Repair requests.
* Hostile machines.
* Allied Builder Drones.
* Salvage Leech attachments.

They should not all behave identically.

⸻

7.2 Something Behind You

The hallway produces heavy footsteps and machinery sounds.

No enemy is initially visible.

Sequence

1. Present audio or text warning.
2. Allow a short reaction window.
3. Check whether the player scans, prepares, or continues.
4. Reveal a damaged mechanical enemy behind the party.

Player options

Prepare an Ambush

* Spend an action.
* Gain a first-attack advantage if the enemy appears.

Scan the Hallway

* Reveal the enemy’s general threat class.
* May expose its damaged component.

Keep Moving

* Preserve resources.
* Risk enemy initiative.

Hide or Avoid

* Attempt to bypass the enemy using existing evasion rules, if supported.

Enemy special feature

The enemy retains visible signs of previous combat.

It may have:

* Missing armor.
* Broken weapon mounts.
* Damaged sensors.
* Reduced movement.
* A damaged or malfunctioning power system.

Do not invent a prior encounter as established history. If the game records earlier enemies, reuse actual state where possible; otherwise generate an explicitly separate damaged enemy.

⸻

7.3 The Salvage Room

The player discovers a chamber full of disassembled industrial machinery and valuable components.

A large salvage cache lies near an inactive defense system.

Player options

Take Everything

* Gain 3–5 Scrap.
* Activate a defensive encounter or trap.
* Reward should justify the added risk.

Take a Little

* Gain 2 Scrap.
* Chance of activating a minor hazard.
* Lower risk than taking everything.

Leave It Alone

* Gain a safe 1 Scrap from accessible debris.
* Avoid the defense encounter.

Use a Builder Drone

* Send the drone to sort or examine the cache.
* Potentially identify a trap.
* Increase salvage efficiency when successful.
* Risk drone damage if the defense system activates.

Use a Salvage Leech

* Attach it to a dormant mechanical defense target when eligible.
* It cannot produce extraction value without actual qualifying damage.
* May create an advantage if combat begins.

Reward rules

Each cache must have a fixed reward budget determined at event generation.

Do not permit repeatedly entering and leaving to regenerate its contents.

⸻

7.4 Circular Route

The player follows a hallway and unexpectedly arrives at the same entrance again.

The room resembles the previously cleared location.

Sequence

1. Show a recognizable duplicate environment.
2. Preserve the player’s perception of familiar room features.
3. Reveal a damaged enemy or anomaly associated with the room.
4. Defeating or resolving that obstacle ends the loop.

Player options

Fight the Apparition

* Defeat the returned enemy.
* Gain a small additional reward.

Inspect the Environment

* Identify the inconsistency.
* Reveal a shortcut or weaken the anomaly.

Mark the Entrance

* Record a spatial reference.
* Reduce the cost or risk of breaking the loop.

Use a Builder Drone

* Ask it to investigate familiar maintenance features.
* Potentially reveal a hidden exit.

Use a Leech

* Attach to the returning mechanical enemy.
* Extract resources normally within encounter limits.

Continuity rule

If this event references a previously defeated enemy, preserve recognizable damage and missing components.

However, do not revive a unique narrative character or canonically killed entity without explicit permission from the game’s established rules.

The returned enemy may instead be a simulation, duplicate, automated shell, or anomaly.

Its exact nature remains OPEN.

8. Special combined event — The Hallway That Eats Machines

Trigger requirements:

* Wrong Hallway is active.
* An allied Builder Drone is available.
* The player possesses a usable Salvage Leech or compatible variant.
* The special event has not already occurred during the current anomaly.

This event enters the rare Wrong Hallway pool only when its requirements are satisfied.

Scene

The Builder Drone detects a maintenance signal behind a false wall.

Inside is a cavity full of dismantled construction machines.

Several are still operating.

Others are covered in dormant salvage parasites.

The allied Builder Drone attempts to communicate with the abandoned machinery.

Option A — Harvest the Machines

* Gain 4 Scrap.
* Disturb dormant parasites.
* Trigger a short encounter against salvaging machines or an infested defense unit.
* The player can use Leech abilities during the fight.

Option B — Reactivate the Machinery

* Spend 2 Scrap or an equivalent repair resource.
* Activate one temporary friendly Builder Drone.
* The new ally lasts for the remainder of the anomaly encounter.
* There is a chance of awakening one damaged hostile machine.

Option C — Extract the Parasites

* Attempt to recover a usable Leech.
* Success grants a temporary Leech card or refreshes one exhausted instance.
* Failure may cause a parasite to attach to allied machinery.

The temporary reward must expire at the appropriate encounter boundary.

Option D — Seal the Cavity

* Spend 1 Energy or use a compatible engineering action.
* Prevent the dormant machinery from activating.
* Gain a modest safe salvage reward.
* Preserve the allied Builder Drone.

Option E — Retreat

* Abandon special rewards.
* Return to the primary hallway decision.
* Do not create another special event roll.

Additional possible reactions

* A Possessed Builder might automatically attempt to awaken the dormant machines.
* A Scavenger Drone may identify the richest salvage pile.
* A Symbiotic Leech might react to a large maintenance signal.
* A Queen Salvager might provoke a hostile infestation response.
* A Fully Awake Drone can permit a controlled investigation choice.

These are encounter-specific interactions, not new global card powers.

9. Remaining card roster

Implement all nine other previously proposed cards.

9.1 Emergency Bulkhead

Type: Structure
Cost: 2 Scrap
HP: 6

Deploy a temporary protective bulkhead.

Effects

* Blocks attacks aimed at an allied card directly behind it, subject to existing line-of-sight rules.
* Can be damaged and destroyed.
* Does not block attacks that explicitly ignore cover.

Active ability — Emergency Collapse

The player may deliberately destroy the bulkhead.

* Prevent 2 damage to every allied unit in its protected lane for the current resolution window.
* Cannot be activated after damage has already finished resolving.
* Must show the bulkhead collapsing or sealing shut.

Interactions

* Builder Drone can repair the bulkhead if valid.
* Salvage Leech cannot normally attach to friendly structures.
* Maintenance Foam can temporarily protect the bulkhead.
* Explosive area attacks use existing cover rules.

⸻

9.2 Threat Beacon

Type: Dangerous Utility
HP: 5

Deployment

* Immediately gain 4 Scrap.
* Increase Threat substantially; proposed baseline is +2 Threat units if the system uses discrete levels.
* Deploy a hostile-attracting Beacon.

Effects

* Eligible enemies prioritize attacking the Beacon.
* Enemies do not ignore unreachable or invalid targets.
* Area attacks can still affect nearby allies.
* The Beacon cannot repeatedly generate Scrap from repair, redeployment, or recovery of the same instance.

Possibilities

* A Builder Drone can repair it.
* Enemy salvage machines may attempt to dismantle it.
* Enemy sensors may detect the Beacon immediately.
* Beacon destruction produces a distinct warning and animation.

If Threat is continuous rather than discrete, convert the +2 value to a configurable equivalent.

⸻

9.3 Captured Servo Limb

Type: Equipment
Target: Character

Effects

* The first melee attack made each turn receives +2 damage.
* At high Madness, the limb may produce an additional involuntary swing.

Proposed malfunction rules

* High Madness threshold: 5 on an assumed 0–10 scale.
* Malfunction chance: 20% after the empowered attack.
* Choose a random valid nearby target, potentially including an ally if friendly fire is supported.
* Do not generate another malfunction from the extra swing.

Options

* Unequip the limb outside the active attack sequence.
* Use existing stabilizing abilities to lower Madness.
* Retain the limb for additional damage despite the risk.

If the game uses a different Madness range, scale thresholds accordingly.

⸻

9.4 Cold Reactor Cell

Type: Rare Resource / Equipment
Charges: 3

Effect

Spend one Charge instead of 1 Energy when activating an eligible Tech ability.

The Cell cannot pay multiple Energy unless an ability explicitly permits multiple Charge spending.

Empty-cell outcomes

When its final Charge is consumed, roll one discharge outcome:

* 60%: Safe discharge.
* 25%: Recover 1 Scrap.
* 15%: Reactor burst dealing 2 damage to nearby eligible units.

These values are proposed tuning defaults.

Interactions

* Does not refill automatically through ordinary repair.
* Cannot be duplicated through salvage triggers.
* Reactor burst uses existing area-damage rules.
* Provide a clear warning before the final Charge is spent.

⸻

9.5 Machine Possum

Type: Machine Ally
HP: 3
Attack: 0
Proposed cost: 1 Scrap

Behavior — Play Dead

When directly attacked:

1. Resolve the first incoming hit.
2. If still alive, enter shutdown mode.
3. Become unavailable as a direct attack target for the rest of that turn.

Area damage still applies according to normal rules.

Salvage behavior

When an enemy dies while the Possum is active:

* Wake up if shut down.
* Collect 1 Scrap worth of salvage from the corpse.
* Store it as pending room rewards.
* Maximum 3 bonus Scrap per encounter.

Restrictions

* Cannot harvest the same corpse more than once.
* If destroyed before reward collection, unrecovered carried salvage follows normal dropped-item rules.
* Does not steal already allocated unique quest loot.

Personality

Give it a distinctive fake-shutdown pose, twitching limbs, and frantic scavenging animations.

⸻

9.6 Floor-Crawler Gun Nest

Type: Deployable Tech
HP: 5
Attack: 2
Proposed cost: 2 Scrap

Autonomous behavior

* Select nearest eligible enemy.
* Move one valid position toward it per turn.
* Attack when in range.
* Do not voluntarily retreat.
* Recalculate target if its current target dies.

Clamp behavior

When it reaches an eligible enemy:

* Clamp onto that target or adjacent valid contact position.
* Increase damage against that target by 2.
* Remain attached until target destruction, removal, or Gun Nest destruction.

If the movement system does not permit shared occupancy, represent the clamp as an attachment status rather than placing both units in one tile.

Special interactions

* Builder Drone can repair the Gun Nest.
* Enemies may attempt to shake it loose.
* Leech can coexist with the clamp if attachment rules allow distinct slots.
* Gun Nest must not attack twice simply because clamping occurs during its movement phase.

⸻

9.7 Red-Mouth Ammunition

Type: Nightmare Equipment / Consumable

Effect

* Add +3 damage to a firearm attack.
* Gain 1 Madness for each use.
* Consume one ammunition use according to existing equipment rules.

High-Madness malfunction

At high Madness, a shot may fire before the player finalizes the target.

Proposed chance: 20%.

Target selection

* Use valid weapon-range targets.
* Prefer the player’s initially highlighted target when malfunction is not triggered.
* During malfunction, choose randomly among eligible targets.
* Friendly-fire risk applies only if compatible with existing combat rules.

The ammunition should visibly show metallic tooth-like seams, abnormal heat, or a brief recoil disturbance.

Provide a warning before a dangerous automatic discharge.

⸻

9.8 Maintenance Foam

Type: Utility
Cost: 1 Energy

Friendly use

Cover one eligible friendly card with foam.

For one turn:

* It cannot act.
* It cannot be directly targeted.
* At effect expiration, restore 2 HP.
* Area hazards follow existing environmental damage rules.

Enemy use

Attempt to disable an eligible enemy for one turn.

Proposed resistance:

* Ordinary enemies: no resistance unless specified by their traits.
* Heavy enemies: 50% resistance.
* Bosses: 75% resistance, or complete immunity only when explicitly tagged.

Restrictions

* Cannot permanently lock enemies.
* Applying foam repeatedly must consume resources.
* Cannot heal destroyed cards.
* Existing foam removal and duration rules take precedence.

Presentation

Show the target encased in expanding industrial foam, followed by cracking, shedding, or dissolution when the effect expires.

⸻

9.9 Dead Pilot’s Reflex

Type: Nightmare Reaction

Trigger

An allied machine is destroyed.

Effect

* Jubey immediately performs one attack based on the destroyed machine’s printed basic attack damage.
* Gain 1 Madness.
* Resolve as a reaction, not a new full turn.

Restrictions

* Once per turn.
* Requires a valid target.
* Does not copy special abilities, salvage effects, or recursive death triggers.
* Cannot trigger from a destroyed temporary effect that lacks a printed attack.
* Does not retroactively prevent the ally’s destruction.

Presentation

Briefly display an involuntary movement or combat reflex echoing the lost machine.

Do not imply that this represents a new canon ability without further story approval.

10. Cross-card synergy and special cases

Implement meaningful but bounded interactions.

Combination	Interaction
Builder Drone + Emergency Bulkhead	Drone repairs damaged cover
Builder Drone + Threat Beacon	Drone keeps the decoy functional
Builder Drone + Gun Nest	Drone repairs the deployed weapon
Builder Drone + Symbiotic Leech	Successful repairs generate bonus Scrap
Builder Drone + Maintenance Foam	Drone can repair eligible targets after foam expires
Builder Drone + Wrong Hallway	Unlocks scouting and maintenance choices
Salvage Leech + Gun Nest	Gun Nest damage contributes toward extraction
Salvage Leech + damaged heavy enemy	Creates a salvage opportunity but provokes removal attempts
Queen Salvager + multiple enemies	Can transfer pressure through limited offspring deployment
Machine Possum + Salvage Leech	Possum collects corpse salvage while Leech rewards qualifying damage
Threat Beacon + Wrong Hallway	Extra enemies may target the Beacon if it remains deployed
Cold Reactor Cell + Wrong Hallway	Can substitute eligible Energy spending when applicable
Dead Pilot’s Reflex + destroyed Builder Drone	Jubey can perform the drone’s printed 1-damage attack
Red-Mouth Ammunition + Captured Servo Limb	Supports a high-risk Madness build without allowing both effects to enhance the same nonmatching attack
Maintenance Foam + damaged allied machine	Temporarily protects and heals the machine

Interaction safeguards

Do not introduce hidden special effects simply because two card names appear together.

Each synergy should resolve through ordinary card rules unless explicitly authored as a special encounter.

Display contextual combat logs, such as:

* “Builder Drone repaired Gun Nest: +2 HP”
* “Symbiotic Leech harvested maintenance scrap: +1”
* “Salvage Leech stripped armor: -1”
* “Threat Beacon attracted enemy targeting”
* “Wrong Hallway: additional encounter unlocked”

11. New encounter and AI behaviors

Introduce reusable enemy behavior tags:

* mechanical
* repair_capable
* salvage_sensitive
* leech_purge
* defensive
* aggressive
* neutral_maintenance
* heavy_machine
* anomaly_eligible

Use compatibility adapters if equivalent tags already exist.

Enemy decisions

Mechanical enemies should evaluate:

1. Immediate lethal threats.
2. Available attacks.
3. Attached parasites.
4. Damaged components.
5. Repair opportunities.
6. Environmental targets.
7. Threat Beacon distraction.
8. Escape or defensive behavior.

Do not have every enemy react to the Leech.

Some should ignore it.

Others should panic, attempt removal, or seek repairs.

This distinction is important for enemy variety.

Environmental reactions

Allow room machinery to react to:

* Repair attempts.
* Attacking neutral machines.
* Repeated salvage collection.
* Reactivated maintenance systems.
* Defensive alarms.
* Destroyed structures.
* Anomalous navigation effects.

Whenever a reaction will substantially increase danger, provide an observable warning or a clearly risky player choice.

⸻

12. UX requirements

Card readability

Each card should clearly show:

* Cost.
* HP and damage, where applicable.
* Activation timing.
* Remaining charges or extraction limits.
* Current target.
* Relevant Madness or Threat risks.
* Upgrade identity.

Avoid hiding important rules in flavor text.

Field feedback

* Builder Drone: repairing, gathering, confused, following wreckage.
* Salvage Leech: attached, extracting, stripping armor, being purged.
* Gun Nest: moving, aiming, clamping.
* Threat Beacon: broadcasting and attracting enemies.
* Machine Possum: playing dead and collecting salvage.
* Wrong Hallway: exit inconsistency, event warning, decision screen.

Wrong Hallway interface

Display:

* Current anomaly title.
* Brief environmental description.
* Available choices.
* Costs and risks.
* Known rewards.
* Whether a Builder Drone option is available.
* Whether the player has already searched or marked the corridor.

Unknown outcomes should remain concealed unless revealed by scanning or scouting.

⸻

13. Balance and exploit prevention

Scrap economy

Apply per-encounter limits where specified.

Important caps:

* Base Salvage Leech: 3 extractions per attachment.
* Symbiotic Leech: 2 bonus Scrap per encounter.
* Machine Possum: 3 bonus Scrap per encounter.
* Queen Salvager: 1 temporary offspring per attachment.
* Wrong Hallway: one additional extension encounter per room.

The Builder Drone’s repeated Scrap generation must remain constrained by its action interval, survivability, and ordinary encounter length.

Combat difficulty

Extra salvage should usually require at least one of:

* Additional danger.
* Energy expenditure.
* Turn expenditure.
* Equipment vulnerability.
* Increased Threat.
* A difficult navigation choice.

Randomness

Use deterministic seeded rolls.

Roll results should be associated with the triggering action, not recalculated every render frame.

Avoid changing previously determined outcomes when the player opens and closes an event screen.

Encounter protection

* Prevent recursive Wrong Hallway events.
* Prevent infinite repair/extraction loops.
* Prevent repeatedly claiming a cache.
* Prevent unlimited temporary card creation.
* Prevent unavailable targets from receiving automatic attacks.
* Prevent disappearance of the only exit.
* Permit safe recovery from interrupted exploration events.

16. Acceptance criteria

This expansion is ready for an initial playable test when:

1. All 12 base cards have working effects.
2. All seven named upgrade/variant paths are functional.
3. Builder Drone autonomously repairs or salvages.
4. Salvage Leech extracts resources and responds correctly to host damage, death, and removal.
5. Wrong Hallway can generate each of its four main events.
6. Every listed main navigation option has a working outcome.
7. The special combined event can trigger under its defined requirements.
8. Each event has a valid completion or retreat path.
9. Card effects cannot generate infinite resource loops.
10. New abilities have useful visual and textual feedback.
11. Existing deck, combat, and room progression remain intact.
12. Automated or reproducible tests cover critical edge cases.

OPEN / TUNE LATER

The implementation should expose these values or decisions without silently declaring them canon:

* Exact card costs where not previously specified.
* Madness thresholds and malfunction chances.
* Threat increase from deploying a Beacon.
* Drone malfunction frequency.
* Wrong Hallway event weights.
* Salvage payout amounts.
* Leech attachment compatibility and enemy immunities.
* Whether any Leech variant can be permanently destroyed in harder modes.
* How often the two special events appear.
* Whether the returning enemy in Circular Route is a duplicate, simulation, or actual anomaly.
* Whether additional card variants are separate collectible cards or permanent upgrades.
* Final compatibility with the current Mechanica Descent ruleset.

Final design target: Make the player feel that scavenged machinery is increasingly helpful, increasingly dangerous, and increasingly difficult to distinguish from the facility’s own hostile systems.

The Builder Drone should be endearing and occasionally alarming. The Salvage Leech should make damaged enemies economically valuable without trivializing combat. Wrong Hallway should turn an ordinary successful encounter into a memorable new decision.

Together, these systems should support an entire salvage-oriented playstyle while enriching the atmosphere of Mechanica Descent.
