MECHANICA DESCENT

Capture Cards, Containment & The Self-Repairing Prisoner

Detailed Implementation Brief

Status: PROPOSED — new content for implementation, not confirmation of existing features.

Scope: Six selected cards and encounters, capture-state logic, resistance and escape, prisoner presentation, pilot hazards, intelligence extraction, Improvised Army integration, and validation.

⸻

1. Implementation goals

Expand Mechanica Descent with nonlethal capture and mechanical containment as an alternative to destroying enemies.

Selected additions:

1. Clampjaw Drone
2. Industrial Lockbands
3. Cockpit Wedge
4. Mobile Containment Pod
5. Memory Tap
6. The Prisoner That Repairs Itself

Capture must be a tactical investment. Players can preserve enemies for intelligence, salvage, intact hardware, or possible conversion, but must manage securing time, limited capacity, resistance, rescue attempts, equipment damage, and escape.

The Prisoner That Repairs Itself serves as the flagship encounter, with unusually expressive but thoroughly muted behavior.

Explicit decisions for this implementation

LOCKED FOR THIS BRIEF:

* Clampjaw Drone may hold one captive at a time, never several simultaneously.
* Mobile Containment Pod also holds exactly one eligible captive.
* Cockpit Wedge applies only to actual cockpits on piloted machines.
* Cockpit Wedge does not block, intercept, or interfere with communications or transmissions.
* Cockpit Wedge prevents ejection. Therefore, a pilot may remain trapped in a mech that explodes.
* Cockpit Wedge does not automatically prevent reactor destruction, self-destruct, restarting, or weapons operation.
* The Prisoner That Repairs Itself is thoroughly muted: no intelligible speech, only muffled mechanical/vocal-like sounds and ellipsis-based screen expressions.
* The prisoner’s actions must be visibly and audibly foreshadowed, allowing counterplay.

Numerical values below are initial tuning proposals unless otherwise identified.

⸻

2. Shared capture rules

2.1 Target states

Use existing status systems where compatible.

Active

* Normal enemy behavior.
* Can attack, maneuver, defend, and employ special abilities.

Weakened

* Meets a capture tool’s eligibility condition.
* May still attack or resist.
* Not automatically captured.

Disabled

* Unable to perform normal combat actions due to a shutdown or equivalent established effect.
* May still possess restart, escape, or rescue options.

Being Secured

* A capture procedure is underway.
* The captor is committed to the procedure.
* Interruption may cancel progress.

Secured

* Restraints prevent normal combat participation.
* Can attempt specific escape or resistance actions.
* Can be transferred to suitable containment.

Contained

* Stored in a secure device.
* No ordinary combat participation.
* Containment integrity, power, and target-specific escape systems remain relevant.

Escaped

* Restraints or containment failed.
* Enemy returns to its appropriate combat, flight, or recovery behavior.

Distinguish pilot custody from chassis custody. An immobilized mech with a free, active pilot is not necessarily a secured pilot.

2.2 Capture eligibility

Initial supported targets:

Target	Capture requirements
Small autonomous drone	Weakened or disabled; normal restraints
Medium autonomous machine	Weakened, disabled, or controlled; appropriate restraint
Piloted mech	Stabilize chassis and separately address cockpit/pilot
Heavy combat frame	Excluded from ordinary small-target tools unless specially supported
Self-repairing anomaly	Encounter-specific restraint and regeneration logic

A target’s HP alone must not imply that the target has surrendered.

2.3 Escape model

Use a readable Restraint Integrity value rather than unexplained random releases.

Proposed starting scale: 0–100.

* 76–100: Stable restraint
* 41–75: Strained
* 1–40: Critical
* 0: Restraint broken

Different captive types apply different strain.

Restraint actions can restore integrity, while struggle actions reduce it.

Show the current band to the player, with precise numbers optionally available in expanded inspection.

Do not perform arbitrary hidden instant escapes from fully stable restraints.

2.4 Capture outcomes

After sufficiently securing a target, offer compatible options:

* Hold.
* Transfer to containment.
* Extract technical intelligence.
* Recover or dismantle chassis.
* Attempt supervised conversion, where explicitly supported.
* Release, abandon, or withdraw.

Avoid creating unique loot twice through capture followed by destruction.

3. Card 1 — Clampjaw Drone

Type: Common Machine Ally
Cost: 2 Scrap
HP: 4
Attack: 0
Capacity: One captive

Core ability — Apprehend

The drone uses heavy clamps to secure a weakened small or medium mechanical target.

Default eligibility: target at 3 HP or less, or already Disabled.

Capture sequence

Action 1: Grapple

* Move into valid restraint range.
* Engage the target with hydraulic clamps.
* Apply Being Secured.
* Display a clear capture-progress indicator.

Action 2: Lock

* If the grapple remained valid, complete the restraint.
* Target becomes Secured.
* Drone enters Holding state.

Both stages use the drone’s ordinary action opportunities. The sequence does not complete instantly on deployment.

One-captive rule

Once Clampjaw holds a captive:

* It cannot capture another.
* It does not automatically abandon the first captive to pursue a weaker enemy.
* It may hold, reposition, reinforce, transfer, or release its current captive.
* Transfer to containment frees the drone to capture again.
* Drone destruction ends its mechanical hold; independently applied Lockbands remain in place.

Do not implement multigrab upgrades or capacity increases as part of this brief.

Commands

Apprehend: Pursue one selected valid target.

Hold: Maintain current captive and defensive position.

Reinforce: Spend an action increasing restraint integrity.

Transfer: Deliver captive to an available compatible Containment Pod.

Release: Disengage intentionally.

Retreat: Withdraw when not attached to a captive.

Struggle behavior

A secured target may strain against the drone.

Possible consequences:

* Reduce restraint integrity.
* Deal 1 damage to Clampjaw.
* Force the drone to spend its next action reinforcing.
* Cause partial separation without immediately freeing the target.

Heavy or specially powerful targets are ineligible rather than being silently guaranteed to fail.

Industrial Lockbands synergy

Applying Lockbands during the Grapple stage may finish the securing process using the player’s equipment action.

This costs a real action and the Lockbands resource.

Additional Lockbands reinforce the captive after securing.

Builder Drone synergy

Builder Drone can repair Clampjaw while it maintains a captive.

Repair does not automatically restore restraint integrity; reinforcement remains a separate operation.

Visual details

* Short, heavily armored body.
* Two oversized primary clamp arms.
* Smaller alignment claws.
* Distinctive teal eye or sensor.
* Visible clamp pressure indicators.
* Struggling targets shake the drone’s arms.
* At critical restraint integrity, clamps visibly begin separating.

The captive’s resistance should feel physically believable.

4. Card 2 — Industrial Lockbands

Type: Common Utility / Equipment
Cost: 1 Scrap per application
Maximum: 2 bands per eligible captive

Core ability — Secure Target

Apply heavy industrial restraint bands to a Disabled, Being Secured, or Secured target.

First band

* Supplies a basic restraint layer.
* Can complete an eligible grapple.
* Reduces ordinary mobility and restart opportunities.
* Adds 30 proposed Restraint Integrity, capped at 100.

Second band

* Provides redundant restraint.
* Adds 30 additional Integrity.
* Reduces the effectiveness of ordinary struggle attempts.
* Makes enemy rescue operations require additional work.

A second band does not create absolute immunity to breakout.

Usage details

Each band application:

* Consumes a player action.
* Requires a valid target and access.
* Has an immediately visible effect.
* Can be interrupted before completion by applicable enemy responses.

Bands may restrain a mechanical frame, exposed arms, or compatible pilot equipment. They do not automatically restrain a pilot who remains inaccessible inside a sealed cockpit.

Escape interactions

Ordinary enemies strain against the restraint.

Powerful enemies may:

* Tear at a band.
* Overload its locking mechanism.
* Twist their chassis against it.
* Use external cutting equipment.
* Request rescue from allied units.

Damaged bands must show strain warnings.

Capture synergies

Clampjaw Drone: Reinforces the active hold.

Mobile Containment Pod: Provides additional physical security, but does not increase pod capacity.

The Prisoner That Repairs Itself: Delays breakout, particularly if applied before its regeneration track advances.

Improvised Army: Builder Drones may repair compatible restraints when those are represented as damageable equipment.

5. Card 3 — Cockpit Wedge

Type: Uncommon Attachment
Cost: 1 Scrap
Target: A real, accessible cockpit on a piloted mech
Duration: 2 turns

Core ability — Block Ejection

Install a mechanical wedge that prevents an eligible cockpit’s normal opening or emergency ejection mechanism from operating.

The wedge is a physical obstruction, not an electronic jammer.

Exact effects

Blocked:

* Cockpit ejection.
* Normal cockpit opening.
* Mechanical emergency escape through the affected cockpit hatch.

Not blocked:

* Radio communication.
* Distress transmissions.
* Communications with allies.
* Reactor self-destruct.
* Detonation.
* Weapons operation.
* Engine or control-system restart.
* Other abilities unrelated to cockpit opening.

Do not give Cockpit Wedge communications-jamming properties.

The dangerous tradeoff — Trapped Pilot

If the mech becomes critically unstable while its cockpit is wedged, the pilot may be unable to escape.

For example:

1. Player disables a piloted mech.
2. Player installs Cockpit Wedge.
3. Pilot attempts ejection.
4. Ejection fails.
5. Pilot remains inside the mech.
6. Reactor instability worsens.
7. If the mech explodes before the Wedge is removed, the pilot is exposed to the explosion and may be killed.

Do not automatically protect the pilot from blast damage.

Resolve the outcome using established cockpit protection, reactor damage, and pilot survivability rules where available.

If those rules are absent, use an explicit, testable pilot-survival calculation rather than silently granting safety.

Warning feedback

When a wedged cockpit is attached to a critically unstable mech, show warnings such as:

* COCKPIT EJECTION BLOCKED
* REACTOR INSTABILITY DETECTED
* PILOT STILL INSIDE
* DETONATION RISK: HIGH

The player should understand the consequence before taking an additional destructive action.

Pilot reactions

Pilots can:

* Attempt ejection and discover it is blocked.
* Transmit a distress call.
* Ask allies for assistance.
* Attempt to restart the mech.
* Attempt to pry the cockpit mechanism.
* Prepare emergency systems.
* React to imminent reactor destruction.

Radio dialogue may become increasingly desperate.

The Wedge does not stop them from speaking or transmitting.

Player choices

Maintain Wedge

* Continue blocking ejection.
* Preserve the possibility of recovering the intact mech and pilot.
* Accept increasing risk if the chassis is unstable.

Remove Wedge

* Spend an action to disengage.
* Restore normal cockpit opening/ejection behavior if the underlying systems still work.

Extract Pilot

* Requires an appropriate controlled extraction procedure.
* The Wedge must be disengaged or explicitly bypassed.
* Pilot must then be independently secured.

Recover Chassis

* Stabilize and transport the mech if compatible recovery systems are available.

Compatibility restrictions

Cannot target:

* Autonomous drones with no cockpit.
* External machinery without an actual cockpit.
* Unpiloted combat frames.
* Synthetic prisoner machinery merely resembling a humanoid torso.
* The base version of The Prisoner That Repairs Itself, unless an authored variant establishes a piloted cockpit.

Cockpit Wedge is deliberately specialized.

6. Card 4 — Mobile Containment Pod

Type: Rare Structure
Cost: 3 Scrap
HP: 7
Capacity: One captive
Power upkeep: 1 Energy per 3 turns

Core ability — Contain

Transfer one compatible Secured captive into the Pod.

Supported initial targets:

* One captured pilot.
* One small autonomous machine.
* One medium support machine that fits its capacity.

No simultaneous second captive, even if both are small.

Heavy combat frames cannot fit.

An entire piloted combat mech cannot be placed inside simply because its cockpit is wedged. The pilot must be separately extracted and secured, or the chassis must be recovered through a suitable larger system.

Transfer process

1. Confirm the target is Secured.
2. Confirm Pod is vacant and has sufficient integrity.
3. Spend a transfer action.
4. Move the captive into containment.
5. Release temporary field restraints only when transfer succeeds.
6. Mark Pod as Occupied.
7. Preserve any compatible Lockbands.

If transfer is interrupted, the captive remains in its earlier state.

Maintenance cycle

After every three occupied turns, charge 1 Energy.

If paid:

* Maintain containment integrity.

If unpaid:

* Enter Low Power.
* Reduce security integrity.
* Disable some containment protections.
* Begin clear failure warnings.

A single missed payment does not automatically release the captive.

Allow power restoration before catastrophic failure.

Contained captive actions

Most captives may:

* Remain quiet.
* Test restraints.
* Attempt an escape.
* Attempt to manipulate the player through permitted dialogue or signals.
* Damage equipment if their abilities permit.
* React to nearby allies or enemies.

Captured machines should have behavior profiles rather than identical escape rolls.

Pod destruction

If destroyed:

* Release a surviving captive into the appropriate location.
* Apply normal environmental consequences.
* Resolve whether the captive can move, attack, flee, or remains independently restrained.
* Preserve Lockbands unless the destruction also damages them.

Do not automatically convert every freed captive into an immediately attacking enemy.

Available processing options

Hold: Keep captive contained.

Memory Tap: Extract compatible technical data.

Dismantle: Process a captured machine into permitted salvage.

Transfer: Move the captive to a supported storage or route system.

Supervised Conversion: Available only for explicitly eligible machines.

Release: Remove captive from custody through a controlled action.

Builder Drone interaction

A Builder Drone can:

* Repair the Pod.
* Reinforce compatible mechanisms.
* Diagnose power failure.
* Assist safe transfer.

Do not permit normal HP repair to substitute for Energy upkeep.

7. Card 5 — Memory Tap

Type: Uncommon Utility / Intelligence
Cost: 1 Energy
Target: Secured or Contained compatible machine

Core ability — Extract Diagnostic Intelligence

Read intact machine memory, operating records, sensor logs, and navigation traces.

It does not automatically extract thoughts from a human pilot.

For piloted machines, it accesses the chassis and cockpit’s electronic records, not the pilot’s private mind.

Three choices

Hazard Scan

Reveal an eligible nearby danger.

Possible outputs:

* Known trap location.
* Ambush signature.
* Hazard category.
* Machinery activation warning.

Weakness Analysis

Reveal one useful weakness of a known or relevant upcoming enemy.

Possible outputs:

* Vulnerable armor component.
* Damaged subsystem.
* Resistance limitation.
* Weapon startup behavior.

Cache Trace

Identify a potential salvage location.

Possible outputs:

* Maintenance storage.
* Hidden component locker.
* Discarded robot parts.
* Accessible salvage room.

Do not create a guaranteed cache without a valid encounter or reward location to attach it to.

Data quality

Proposed initial table:

Result	Base probability
Clean	60%
Partial	30%
Corrupted	10%

Clean: Accurate, specific information.

Partial: Accurate but incomplete information.

Corrupted: Unreliable or contradictory information, clearly identified as questionable by the interface.

Quality modifiers may include:

* ●	reliability for contained targets.
* ●	reliability for intact sensors and storage.
* ●	reliability when an authorized cockpit connection is available.
* ●	reliability for severe internal damage.
* ●	reliability for active counterintrusion behavior.
* ●	reliability for anomalous prisoner machines.

Avoid fabricated certainty. Corrupted results should have discoverable inconsistencies rather than creating completely unavoidable traps.

Repeated use

Each captive provides a bounded intelligence budget.

Proposed:

* One full-value extraction per captive.
* Subsequent attempts may reveal additional fragments only when the source explicitly has unrecovered information.
* No infinite rewards through repeated taps.

Possible backlash

A corrupted machine may:

* Attempt to transmit an alert if physically able.
* Damage the connection.
* Display misleading data.
* Activate a dormant defense.
* Increase its escape pressure.

A captive’s communication capabilities must be checked separately from Cockpit Wedge status.

8. Rare Nightmare Encounter — The Prisoner That Repairs Itself

8.1 Identity and presentation

A damaged autonomous machine is discovered inside an old containment station.

Its body is covered in reinforced restraint bands.

Its main voice system is completely muted.

Yet it is still moving.

Small mechanisms click underneath its armor. Sealed actuators strain. Broken components slowly realign.

Its display shows nothing resembling normal speech.

Only ellipses, punctuation, and brief changes in rhythm.

The prisoner has no ordinary intelligible voice lines.

Its silence is a defining feature of the encounter.

8.2 Visual design

Suggested appearance:

* Tall, battered mechanical captive.
* Thick restraints around torso, limbs, and internal articulations.
* Damaged faceplate or single expression screen.
* Limited head and shoulder motion.
* Internal repair mechanisms beneath the plating.
* Visible strain at the joints when attempting escape.
* Intermittent light leaking through damaged seams.
* Screen displaying ellipsis-based expressions.

The machine should look contained, but not inactive.

Expression screen

Its screen may display:

* …
* … …
* ……
* …?
* …!
* ……!
* …!!

The expressions are not normal dialogue. They are incomplete, almost accidental signs of internal intent.

Use different pauses, light intensity, animation timing, and punctuation to distinguish behaviors.

8.3 Thoroughly muted audio behavior

The prisoner attempts to make sounds but cannot produce understandable words.

Permitted sounds:

* Soft, muffled “mmph”-like mechanical vocalizations.
* Air pressure pushing through sealed housings.
* Strained electronic hums.
* Quiet metallic rattling.
* Short frustrated vibrations.
* Small motor clicks.
* Low, contained grinding.
* A sharp muffled burst during an escape attempt.

No clear speech should come from the prisoner, even when enraged.

The sounds should feel trapped behind sealed machinery.

Important distinction: Its voice output is muted; its physical machinery is not silent.

Players must be able to hear mechanical movement and recognize dangerous activity.

9. Prisoner repair and escape progression

Use a five-stage sequence with distinct audiovisual cues.

Stage 0 — Apparently Quiet

Screen: …

Audio: Occasional low mechanical hum.

Animation: Minimal movement; slow repairs beneath armor.

The prisoner appears subdued.

Small warning signs suggest it may still be functioning.

Stage 1 — Testing the Restraints

Screen: … …

Audio: Repeated small metallic clicks and muffled mechanical murmuring.

Animation: Fingers flex. Shoulders shift. A restraint tightens.

Gameplay: Repair progress advances.

The player can inspect or reinforce the restraints before the situation worsens.

Stage 2 — Calculating an Escape

Screen: ……

Audio: Rhythmic pressure pulses, increasingly deliberate.

Animation: One restraint begins visibly bending.

Gameplay: The prisoner prepares a specific escape or sabotage action.

Its body movement should reveal which area is under strain.

Stage 3 — Imminent Action

Screen: …!

Audio: A longer muffled mechanical protest, followed by a sharp clamp noise.

Animation: The prisoner visibly braces or twists.

Gameplay: Its next scheduled action attempts to break restraint, sabotage equipment, or trigger another preselected hostile behavior.

Provide the player a clear response window.

Stage 4 — Breakout

Screen: …!!

Audio: Loud metallic snapping, mechanical pressure release, and a final muffled vocal-like burst.

Animation: Restraints fail or a component escapes its enclosure.

Gameplay: Resolve the prepared action.

Possible outcomes:

* Partial breakout.
* Fully escaped machine.
* Containment damage.
* A damaged player machine.
* A recapture opportunity.

Breakout should not always mean instantaneous full freedom.

The exact outcome depends on target state, restraint integrity, applied equipment, and prior player actions.

10. Prisoner intent and readable tells

In addition to its repair stage, assign a current intent.

These are important because the ellipses should cue what the prisoner is actually about to do.

Intent	Screen cue	Sound / animation	Possible response
Self-repair	Slow …	Repeated internal welding clicks	Interrupt repair or accelerate capture
Restraint break	Fast … …!	Clamp strain, shoulder twist	Reinforce restraints
Feigned compliance	Long, still ……	Mechanical activity suddenly stops	Inspect before trusting
Pod sabotage	Pulsing …!	Tapping near Pod controls	Repair or isolate controls
Data trap	Flickering …?	Sensor clicks near Memory Tap connection	Disconnect or verify records
Desperate escape	Bright …!!	Rapid, uneven rattling	Immediate containment action

The same symbol may appear in multiple contexts, but animations and sound must make intent reasonably interpretable.

Do not reveal precise success chances as screen dialogue.

The point is to create uncertainty without making the encounter unreadable.

Feigned compliance

One especially important behavior:

The prisoner suddenly stops struggling.

Its screen goes almost still.

It produces a tiny, muffled sound.

Its restraints appear stable.

This may genuinely mean its repairs have stalled—or it may be waiting for the player to remove equipment or initiate a Memory Tap.

The player can inspect it, reinforce containment, or proceed cautiously.

A successful inspection exposes the deception before the hostile action occurs.

11. Prisoner encounter choices

Option A — Strip It for Parts

The player dismantles useful components.

Proposed reward: 4 Scrap and a chance of a compatible uncommon component.

Risks:

* Repair progress may accelerate during dismantling.
* The prisoner may sabotage the cutting process.
* Valuable memory components may be damaged.
* A partially freed machine may attack or attempt escape.

Allow the player to stop dismantling and secure it again if the action is interrupted.

Option B — Reinforce Containment

Use Industrial Lockbands, compatible support machinery, or a suitable Pod.

Effects:

* Restore restraint integrity.
* Delay or reverse breakout progress.
* Improve chances of safe analysis.
* Consume actions and resources.

Repeated reinforcement is bounded by equipment capacity and integrity limits.

The prisoner should visibly react: its screen may show …! while it attempts to test the new restraints.

Option C — Memory Tap

Attempt to access the prisoner’s diagnostic memory.

Possible rewards:

* An unusual route clue.
* A rare maintenance cache.
* An enemy-machine weakness.
* An important warning about a nearby facility system.

Risks:

* Corrupted or false records.
* Counterintrusion behavior.
* Accelerated escape.
* Temporary malfunction of the Tap interface.

During an attempted data trap, show …? and faint sensor chatter before the hostile response.

The source may be extremely useful but not necessarily trustworthy.

Option D — Recruit Under Supervision

Attempt to convert the prisoner into a temporary allied machine.

This is the highest-risk branch.

Possible requirements:

* Stable containment.
* Adequate repair.
* Successful compatible control-system intervention.
* A limited supervision period.

Possible outcomes:

Controlled Success

* Temporary allied machine, with known limitations.

Unstable Success

* Allied machine with a visible malfunction risk.

Failure

* Prisoner attempts to break free or turns hostile.

Do not grant permanent loyalty solely because it was repaired.

Option E — Leave It Alone

Withdraw without dismantling or interacting further.

* Preserve resources.
* Avoid immediate danger.
* Leave the machine in its current state.

Only advance longer-term consequences if the game already supports revisiting encounters or persistent room events.

12. Special card interactions with the prisoner

Clampjaw Drone

Can attempt to secure the prisoner after it becomes physically eligible.

* Holds exactly one captive.
* May be damaged by unusually strong struggles.
* Can delay partial breakout.
* Cannot overpower an ineligible heavy prisoner merely by existing.

If the prisoner is too large for normal Clampjaw operation, provide a clear incompatibility indication rather than silently applying an ineffective restraint.

Industrial Lockbands

The primary reinforcement tool.

* First band improves security.
* Second band creates redundancy.
* Neither permanently disables self-repair.

Use visible deformation to show that the prisoner is threatening the bands.

Cockpit Wedge

Not applicable to the base prisoner.

The base encounter concerns an autonomous restrained machine without a confirmed cockpit.

Only an explicitly authored piloted variant with an actual cockpit may receive Cockpit Wedge.

Even then, the Wedge blocks ejection, not transmissions or reactor failure.

Mobile Containment Pod

Can contain the prisoner only if its size and form are eligible.

While inside:

* The prisoner may continue repairing.
* Internal damage can threaten Pod integrity.
* Low Power increases breakout danger.
* Maintenance and reinforcement remain relevant.

Do not shrink an oversized prisoner into the Pod for convenience.

Memory Tap

Offers valuable intelligence but may activate counterintrusion behavior.

The prisoner’s screen may shift from … to …? during connection.

If a data trap is pending, the player receives a warning opportunity before it resolves.

Builder Drone

Can:

* Repair compatible containment hardware.
* Diagnose damaged restraints.
* Support stabilizing operations.
* Accidentally assist the prisoner if using an existing malfunction behavior.

Normal Builder Drone repair must not automatically heal a hostile prisoner unless the rules explicitly permit wrong-target repair.

Manual Override Hand

Can correct eligible malfunctions in an allied converted prisoner.

It cannot be used as a free instant capture tool while the prisoner remains hostile.

Scrap Shepherd

Can collect eligible wreckage after the prisoner is legitimately dismantled or destroyed.

It cannot repeatedly harvest the same imprisoned machine.

Black-Box Recorder

If the prisoner becomes an allied machine and is subsequently destroyed, it may satisfy the Recorder’s ordinary allied-machine destruction trigger.

13. Encounter behavior and pacing

The prisoner’s behavior should respond to player decisions, not merely advance a random timer.

Examples:

Player hesitates:

* Repair track advances.
* Sounds become more deliberate.
* Screen ellipses grow more urgent.

Player reinforces:

* Restraint integrity improves.
* Prisoner briefly struggles against the new pressure.
* Repair progress may continue internally.

Player disconnects Memory Tap:

* Data trap is canceled if it has not resolved.
* Prisoner may return to restraint testing.

Player dismantles a repair component:

* Regeneration may slow.
* Prisoner may begin attacking restraints instead.

Player releases a restraint too early:

* Prisoner may exploit the opening.
* A new breakout action becomes available.

Player successfully contains it:

* Its movement becomes limited.
* Display remains active.
* A quiet … may appear after the Pod locks.

Do not make every silent moment a deception. The prisoner should sometimes genuinely stop to conserve effort.

14. Capture archetype integration

The selected cards should support three main strategies.

A. Field Apprehension

Clampjaw Drone + Industrial Lockbands

Capture a weakened machine while combat continues.

Good for small targets and inexpensive salvage or intelligence.

Weakness: Exposed to enemy interference.

B. Piloted-Mech Recovery

Cockpit Wedge + Lockbands + appropriate extraction/recovery tools

Prevent ejection while attempting to capture a pilot and preserve the chassis.

Good for intact mechanical rewards.

Weakness: The pilot remains able to transmit, restart systems, and may be trapped by a subsequent explosion.

The Wedge must never function as automatic safe pilot capture.

C. Secure Intelligence Recovery

Clampjaw Drone + Lockbands + Mobile Containment Pod + Memory Tap

Invest in long-term containment to gain reliable information.

Good for rare technical intelligence and controlled dismantling.

Weakness: Resource-intensive and vulnerable to pod failure.

15. Implementation logic

Capture state validation

Every capture-related action must verify:

* Target exists.
* Target is eligible.
* Target is reachable.
* Target is in the required state.
* Captor has available capacity.
* Cost can be paid.
* Required equipment is present.
* No conflicting state prevents resolution.

One-captive enforcement

For Clampjaw Drone:

heldCaptiveId must be either null or one valid target ID.

For Mobile Containment Pod:

containedCaptiveId must be either null or one valid target ID.

Do not use collection arrays permitting multiple captives in these two card instances.

Transfers should be atomic: never duplicate a captive across the Drone and Pod.

Cockpit Wedge validation

Before attachment, verify:

* isPiloted = true
* hasActualCockpit = true
* cockpitAccessible = true
* target is not already incompatible with wedging

On installation, set only the appropriate hatch/ejection prevention state.

Do not modify:

* communications capability
* radio availability
* distress signal availability
* reactor stability
* self-destruct capability
* general weapon functionality

If ejection is attempted, reject the ejection while the Wedge remains effective.

Explosion damage must continue normally, with the pilot still occupying the cockpit unless a separate valid escape path succeeded.

Prisoner intent tracking

Track:

* Repair stage.
* Restraint integrity.
* Current intended action.
* Whether the intent was telegraphed.
* Remaining response window.
* Current containment location.
* Relevant equipment.
* Counterintrusion status.
* Unique reward claims.

Choose an intended action deterministically according to encounter state and the run’s seeded RNG.

Present audiovisual cues before executing major hostile actions.

Never determine escape success solely from the current display animation.

Reaction ordering

When a major event happens:

1. Validate action and state.
2. Pay resource costs.
3. Apply direct effects.
4. Update restraint and repair progress.
5. Generate any new intent.
6. Present its tell.
7. Allow valid reactions.
8. Resolve the warned action when its scheduled turn arrives.
9. Process destruction, escape, rewards, and cleanup.

Avoid multiple nested breakout or destruction events firing from one machine action.

16. Interface and accessibility

Capture interface

Display:

* Target capture state.
* Current captor.
* Available restraint slots.
* Restraint integrity.
* Current escape warning level.
* Containment capacity.
* Power upkeep timing.
* Available processing choices.

Prisoner display

Give the Prisoner That Repairs Itself a dedicated, expressive screen.

Text on this screen should remain almost exclusively ellipses and punctuation.

Use animations to communicate:

* hesitation
* surprise
* frustration
* calculation
* false calm
* imminent escape

Its vocal-like sounds must remain unintelligible.

Accessibility

Important gameplay cues cannot depend on sound alone.

Provide:

* visual restraint strain warnings
* readable animation states
* optional audio captions such as [muffled motor strain]
* reduced-motion alternatives
* visible imminent-action markers

The player should be able to understand the encounter while playing with audio muted.

17. Testing plan

Clampjaw Drone

* Captures one eligible target.
* Refuses a second target while occupied.
* Releases capacity after valid transfer.
* Maintains capture state when interrupted.
* Correctly loses its mechanical hold when destroyed.
* Doesn’t erase independent Lockbands.

Industrial Lockbands

* First and second applications produce distinct effects.
* Third application is rejected.

20. Acceptance criteria

The package is ready for its first playable test when:

1. All five ordinary capture cards are functional.
2. Captives cannot be duplicated or stored beyond capacity.
3. Clampjaw and Pod each support exactly one captive.
4. Cockpit Wedge applies only to real cockpits and blocks ejection without affecting communications.
5. A trapped pilot remains vulnerable to catastrophic chassis failure.
6. Captures require meaningful action and resource expenditure.
7. Memory Tap makes securing enemies a useful alternative to destroying them.
8. The Prisoner That Repairs Itself has all major encounter branches.
9. The prisoner is thoroughly muted while still producing expressive, muffled mechanical noises.
10. Ellipsis screen expressions telegraph its intentions.
11. Players can recognize and respond to imminent breakout attempts.
12. Existing combat, navigation, salvage, and ally systems continue functioning.

Final design target: Make capture a deliberate tactical process with memorable personalities, resistance, meaningful custody decisions, and dangerous consequences.

The flagship prisoner should feel especially alive through its machinery: initially quiet, then increasingly restless, its little ellipsis display changing while clamps tighten and internal mechanisms begin to move.

The moment its screen changes from … to …!, the player should recognize that something is about to happen—even though the prisoner cannot say a single understandable word.
