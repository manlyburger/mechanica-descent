MECHANICA DESCENT

Pilot Equipment, Progression, Loyalty & New Choice Encounters

Detailed Design and Implementation Plan

Status: PROPOSED
Target: Next Mechanica Descent expansion
Dependencies: Existing pilot captivity, ejection, Mech Team, Commandeered Cockpit, and encounter systems

1. Primary goals

Introduce individual equipment and character progression for captive and allied pilots.

Every pilot should have:

* An independent equipment screen.
* Three starting equipment slots.
* A fourth unlockable slot.
* Randomized Mental, Skill, and Endurance stats.
* A character level and experience progression.
* Persistent loyalty and cooperation information.
* A specialized equipment pool determined by custody and allegiance.
* Individual status, history, and assignment information.

Also introduce three new choice encounter types:

1. Empty-cockpit mech discovery.
2. Suspicious cargo up a ladder.
3. Fleeing pilot encounter.

Retain pilot identity when transferring between captivity, field assignments, and allied status.

⸻

2. Pilot equipment screens

General layout

Selecting a pilot opens a character-management panel containing:

Header

* Portrait or mech-pilot icon.
* Pilot name or identifier.
* Level and XP.
* Origin or former faction.
* Current allegiance and custody.
* Current mech assignment.

Equipment

* Four equipment positions.
* First three available immediately.
* Fourth initially locked.
* Items presented visually in equipped slots.

Character information

* Mental.
* Skill.
* Endurance.
* Current Madness.
* Current HP or condition, where supported.
* Loyalty/cooperation estimate.
* Specializations and known tendencies.

Actions

* Equip.
* Unequip.
* Upgrade slot.
* View stats.
* Inspect loyalty.
* Assign to mech.
* Return to custody or assign another duty.

Keep the interface compact on phones. Use a dedicated pilot panel rather than crowding the combat field.

2.1 Equipment slots

Every pilot begins with exactly three equipment slots.

Slot	Initial availability
Slot 1	Unlocked
Slot 2	Unlocked
Slot 3	Unlocked
Slot 4	Locked — requires 3 Cockpit Parts

A slot accepts one compatible item.

Equipment is held in the individual pilot’s inventory record, not attached to a generic pilot class.

The fourth slot is unlocked separately for each pilot.

It stays unlocked when that pilot changes status between captive and ally.

2.2 Fourth-slot upgrade

Introduce a new material:

Cockpit Parts

* Uncommon random salvage drop from enemies equipped with actual cockpits.
* Suggested initial drop probability: 20% per eligible cockpit recovery.
* Can appear after capturing an intact cockpit or salvaging an eligible cockpit-equipped enemy.
* A given cockpit source can generate only one part-drop opportunity.
* Do not multiply drops because the same pilot ejects, is recaptured, and changes mechs.

Collecting three Cockpit Parts allows the player to unlock one pilot’s fourth slot.

Upgrade action: Spend 3 Cockpit Parts → unlock Slot 4.

Cockpit Parts are stored in the shared resource inventory until spent.

If the pilot later escapes, the upgrade remains attached to that pilot’s record. It cannot be farmed again by recapturing them.

3. Captive equipment categories

Captive equipment is primarily for security, communication management, and controlled interactions.

These are equipment items, not automatic guarantees of obedience.

3.1 Gags

Gags prevent or impair intelligible spoken communication while fitted.

They do not automatically stop electronic radios, automated alarms, remote relays, or text-based communications.

Suggested items:

Item	Gameplay effect
Basic Muffle Wrap	Blocks ordinary spoken alerts; inexpensive and removable
Reinforced Voice Seal	More resistant to removal; blocks vocal interaction while fitted
Acoustic Suppression Mask	Blocks ordinary audible communication and masks muffled vocal noise at distance
Quick-Release Muzzle	Moderate speech suppression, easy for the player to remove before dialogue or negotiation

Gagged captives should retain expressive eyes, head movement, and muffled vocal reactions where appropriate.

If the player needs the pilot to speak to access a facility system or negotiate, the equipment may need to be temporarily removed.

A gag must not be treated as a method of suppressing all possible warnings.

3.2 Restraints

Item	Gameplay effect
Industrial Lockbands	Increases physical restraint security
Reinforced Cuffs	Stronger protection against ordinary escape attempts
Magnetic Ankle Locks	Reduces unauthorized movement
Armored Restraint Harness	Strong restraint but limits transfers and field assignments
Remote Release Shackles	Can be disengaged by the player using a permitted remote command

Allow two compatible restraint items if the slots permit it.

For example, a captive can use two Lockbands, one gag, and one relay device after unlocking the fourth slot.

Do not allow unlimited stacking or multiple incompatible harnesses.

3.3 Relay devices

Relay equipment monitors or controls specific communication channels and status information.

Item	Gameplay effect
Captive Status Relay	Reports restraint damage and escape warnings
Communication Monitor	Detects compatible unauthorized transmissions
Local Signal Isolator	Blocks a defined radio connection while active
Tracking Relay	Tracks a captive during permitted movement or escape
Custody Diagnostic Relay	Warns when restraints or containment systems begin failing

A monitoring device does not automatically prevent the event it detects.

All devices should specify which communication channels or physical signals they actually affect.

Example captive loadout

Captured Needleback Pilot — 4 slots unlocked

* Reinforced Voice Seal
* Industrial Lockbands
* Magnetic Ankle Locks
* Communication Monitor

The pilot is difficult to move without authorization and cannot issue ordinary spoken warnings, but may still resist, exploit an unmonitored electronic channel, or refuse cooperation.

4. Allied pilot equipment categories

Allied pilots have an equipment pool focused on combat, survivability, and tactical support.

A previously captive pilot who becomes a genuine ally can use this equipment pool according to their current status and permissions.

4.1 Weapons

Equipment	Effect
Standard Sidearm	Basic personal ranged attack
Heavy Sidearm	Higher damage, lower accuracy
Compact SMG	Multiple light attacks where the combat engine supports them
Emergency Carbine	Improved combat reliability during dismounted encounters
Shock Pistol	Temporary nonlethal suppression of eligible targets

Use existing personal combat rules where available.

Do not allow a gun to function as an additional mech-mounted weapon simply because the pilot is inside a cockpit.

4.2 Enhancement modules

Module	Effect
Stabilized Targeting	+5% accuracy
Mental Dampener	Reduces Madness-related penalties
Reinforced Suit Padding	Improves survival against personal damage
Reflex Amplifier	Small defensive reaction bonus
Cockpit Synchronizer	Improves compatible piloted-mech performance

Bonuses should use the same caps as stat and equipment calculations.

4.3 Support items

Item	Effect
Emergency Medkit	Restores pilot condition or HP
Portable Scanner	Reveals appropriate encounter information
Repair Toolkit	Assists compatible mech repairs
Salvage Beacon	Improves retrieval of eligible wreckage
Emergency Escape Beacon	Helps locate or recover an ejected allied pilot

Items with consumable charges must track charges per instance.

Example allied loadout

Level 3 Recovery Pilot — 4 slots unlocked

* Standard Sidearm
* Cockpit Synchronizer
* Repair Toolkit
* Emergency Medkit

This pilot is a useful support specialist both inside and outside a mech.

5. Pilot stats and levels

Introduce three primary RPG attributes:

* Mental
* Skill
* Endurance

Each pilot receives randomized base stats.

Higher-level pilots have stronger potential stats, wider generation ranges, and additional progression bonuses.

5.1 Proposed generation ranges

Level	Stat generation range	Additional benefit
1	1–5 per stat	Starting specialization
2	2–7	+1 earned stat point
3	2–9	+1 earned stat point
4	3–11	+1 earned stat point
5	4–12	+1 earned stat point, specialization improvement

These ranges are for generating a pilot at that level.

Existing pilots should never lose stats or be rerolled downward on level-up.

Level-up process

When a pilot gains a level:

1. Increase level.
2. Increase maximum stat potential.
3. Award one assignable stat point.
4. Apply any level milestone benefit.
5. Preserve prior equipment and custody history.

Suggested XP sources:

* Completing combat sorties.
* Successful recovery operations.
* Discovering useful intelligence.
* Completing difficult assignments.
* Surviving hazardous encounters.
* Fulfilling meaningful cooperation agreements.

Simply keeping a captive imprisoned should not generate unlimited XP.

⸻

6. Mental stat

Purpose: Resistance to Madness effects and Nightmare damage.

Mental determines the pilot’s resilience during anomalous or psychologically disruptive encounters.

6.1 Madness effect reduction

Proposed formula:

Madness penalty reduction = 4% × Mental, capped at 40%.

Examples:

Mental	Madness penalty reduction
2	8%
5	20%
8	32%
12	40% (cap)

This reduces the severity of applicable Madness penalties, rather than automatically deleting accumulated Madness.

The pilot’s actual Madness percentage remains separately tracked.

6.2 Nightmare damage resistance

During a Nightmare encounter, Mental also provides a chance to ignore one point of incoming damage.

Proposed formula:

Nightmare resistance chance = 2% × Mental, capped at 24%.

Roll once for each eligible incoming damage event.

A successful roll prevents one point, never more than one.

Mental’s damage resistance is independent of ordinary armor and Endurance.

Nightmare encounters should clearly display the relevant resistance outcome.

⸻

7. Skill stat

Purpose: Accuracy, technical competence, and special encounter choices.

7.1 Accuracy

Proposed formula:

Accuracy bonus = 3% × Skill, capped at +30 percentage points.

Apply only to compatible attacks using a pilot’s accuracy.

The final attack probability must respect the combat engine’s existing minimum and maximum hit limits.

7.2 Skill thresholds

Use Skill checks to unlock unique dialogue, combat, and facility options.

Suggested thresholds:

Skill requirement	Example
4+	Identify a damaged mech component
6+	Operate a specialized control terminal
8+	Repair or stabilize a difficult subsystem
10+	Attempt a rare technical encounter resolution

If a branch requires Skill 8 and the pilot has Skill 6, show the requirement rather than silently failing the action.

Equipment may supply bonuses if explicitly permitted.

7.3 Specialty considerations

A skilled Needleback pilot is not automatically an expert at operating Kilnheart’s heavy drilling frame.

Keep pilot specialties separate from Skill.

High Skill improves general performance, while specialties determine which advanced tasks are compatible.

⸻

8. Endurance stat

Purpose: Physical survivability and resistance to ordinary damage.

8.1 Damage resistance

Proposed formula:

Chance to ignore one damage = 2.5% × Endurance, capped at 30%.

Roll once per eligible damage event.

A successful check prevents one point.

It does not completely negate high-damage attacks.

8.2 Death avoidance

When an incoming event would kill a pilot, perform an Endurance survival check if the event is survivable under existing rules.

Proposed formula:

Death avoidance chance = 3% × Endurance, capped at 36%.

On success:

* Pilot survives at 1 HP or enters the game’s equivalent critical condition.
* Pilot cannot perform another death-avoidance check against the same damage event.
* Apply normal injury and incapacitation consequences.

Catastrophic events explicitly designated unsurvivable do not receive a generic immunity roll.

For example, Cockpit Wedge still prevents ejection. Endurance does not magically eject a pilot from an exploding mech.

8.3 Combined Mental and Endurance defense

During Nightmare encounters, both attributes may contribute.

Resolve damage prevention in this order:

1. Existing armor and cover.
2. Mental Nightmare resistance, if applicable.
3. Endurance damage resistance, if damage remains.
4. Apply remaining damage.
5. Check Endurance death avoidance if lethal and eligible.

Limit the combined attribute-based prevention to one damage point per incoming hit, even if both checks succeed.

This prevents extremely defensive pilots from generating excessive damage immunity.

⸻

9. Loyalty, cooperation, and captive selection

Pilot cooperation must be visible without exposing every hidden betrayal calculation.

9.1 Loyalty bands

Proposed scale: 0–100.

Loyalty	State	Assignment behavior
0–24	Hostile	Refuses most voluntary assignments
25–44	Unwilling	Restricted or negotiation-only assignments
45–64	Partially Willing	Limited tasks, visible concerns and risks
65–84	Cooperative	Regular assignments with remaining risks
85–100	Loyal	Trusted allied assignments, subject to individual commitments

Loyalty is not identical to restraint security.

9.2 Orange-red captive selection barrier

For Hostile or Unwilling captives, place a distinctive orange-red barrier across unavailable assignment choices.

Suggested appearance:

* Translucent orange-red overlay.
* Lock or warning symbol.
* Visible captive portrait behind the overlay.
* Label: UNWILLING — ASSIGNMENT RESTRICTED

Attempting selection opens an explanation:

* Why the assignment is unavailable.
* Current loyalty band.
* What conditions could improve cooperation.
* Estimated time to reach the next useful stage.
* Available negotiation choices.

Important: The barrier applies to voluntary assignments, not the entire pilot management screen.

Players must still be able to inspect or change appropriate containment equipment and manage custody.

9.3 Partially willing captives

For pilots in the Partially Willing band, show a generalized expectation summary.

Example:

Needleback Pilot — Partially Willing

* Likely to perform scouting.
* May refuse attacks against former teammates.
* Possible escape attempt if given a fast mech.
* More reliable with supervision or a fulfilled agreement.
* Loyalty estimate: approximately 2–4 suitable cooperative assignments under current conditions.

Do not expose the exact hidden betrayal roll.

9.4 Loyalty-time estimates

Both hostile/unwilling and partially willing captives require a progression estimate.

Use a projected range based on:

* Current loyalty.
* Pilot personality.
* Previous treatment.
* Existing agreements.
* Current captivity duration.
* Available cooperation opportunities.
* Former team status.

Example estimates:

Hostile Bishop: “Estimated 6–10 successful cooperation opportunities; currently blocked by unresolved faction loyalty.”

Partially Willing Widow Pilot: “Estimated 2–3 successful assignments to reach Cooperative.”

Unwilling Kilnheart Pilot: “No reliable progression under current conditions. Resolve the outstanding conflict before an estimate is possible.”

Do not promise loyalty solely because time passes.

The estimate must update when circumstances materially change.

9.5 Equipment transitions

When a captive becomes allied:

* Preserve character level.
* Preserve stats.
* Preserve fourth-slot upgrade.
* Preserve identity and history.
* Move incompatible captive equipment to the player’s inventory through a valid transition.
* Enable compatible ally equipment.

If an allied pilot becomes hostile again, do not duplicate or erase their equipment. Preserve actual item ownership and resolve custody appropriately.

10. New encounter — Empty-Cockpit Mech

Type: Uncommon choice encounter

The player discovers an unpiloted mech suit inside an industrial maintenance area.

It has an accessible cockpit but no current pilot.

Initial choices

Inspect the Frame

* Reveal chassis condition.
* Identify compatible pilots.
* Examine special equipment.

Assign an Allied Pilot

* Use a compatible available pilot.
* Activate the mech according to its condition.

Assign a Cooperative Captive

* Use existing Commandeered Cockpit mechanics.
* Apply normal betrayal and supervision rules.

Salvage

* Dismantle the machine for Scrap or components.

Leave

* Continue without claiming the frame.

10.1 Uncommon damaged variant

A minority of these encounters contain an already-damaged mech.

Proposed variant probability: 25%.

The damaged mech may have:

* Reduced starting HP.
* One damaged weapon.
* Faulty sensors.
* A weak cockpit seal.
* Unstable movement equipment.
* A repairable reactor problem.

Provide visual evidence of the damage.

Damaged-mech choices

Repair First

* Spend Scrap or use Builder Drone maintenance.
* Improve safety and usability.

Pilot Immediately

* Avoid preparation costs.
* Accept damage and malfunction risk.

Strip for Components

* Gain salvage without activating the chassis.

Inspect Cockpit

* Reveal whether its ejection equipment is functional.

A damaged discovered mech should still be able to become useful if the player makes the necessary repairs.

⸻

11. New encounter — Suspicious Cargo Above

Type: Choice / exploration encounter

The player discovers an elevated storage platform or suspicious cargo container accessible by ladder.

A warning label is damaged or missing.

The contents cannot be identified clearly from below.

Available options

Send an Unmeched Pilot

* Choose an eligible pilot not currently inside a mech.
* Pilot climbs the ladder and inspects the cargo.
* Requires cooperation if the pilot is captive.

Inspect from Below

* Use a compatible scanner or skill check.
* May identify the hazard before sending anyone.

Use an Appropriate Drone

* Send a compatible small mechanical unit instead.
* Avoid putting a pilot directly at risk.
* May require specialized equipment.

Leave the Cargo

* Skip the reward and danger.

11.1 Normal reward

Most containers hold a random item.

Possible rewards:

* Ammunition.
* Scrap.
* Repair equipment.
* Support item.
* Pilot equipment.
* Rare mechanical component.

Use the encounter’s existing loot tier and inventory rules.

11.2 Toxic gas complication

Introduce an uncommon chance that the cargo releases toxic gas when opened.

Proposed chance: 15%.

Consequences:

* Pilot receives a clear exposure warning.
* Apply eligible status or damage effects using existing hazard rules.
* Pilot may lose loyalty if they believe the player knowingly endangered them.
* The item reward may be damaged or unavailable.

Loyalty effect

Suggested initial range:

* Cooperative pilot exposed unexpectedly: -3 to -7 loyalty.
* Partially willing pilot: -5 to -10 loyalty.
* Hostile captive sent into danger: potentially larger resentment, with no assumption that further coercion improves loyalty.

Modifiers:

* Prior warning and voluntary acceptance reduce resentment.
* Concealing a known hazard increases resentment.
* Prompt medical treatment may mitigate the loss.
* A successful Skill inspection can prevent or reveal the danger.

Do not treat every accidental hazard as automatic betrayal.

11.3 Skill checks

Skill 4+: Identify damaged hazard markings.

Skill 6+: Recognize a suspicious pressure-release mechanism.

Skill 8+: Attempt to inspect or retrieve contents without triggering the gas release.

These are proposed branch requirements.

11.4 Visual feedback

When the hazard triggers:

* Cargo lid opens.
* Gas sprays outward.
* Pilot recoils and moves away.
* Display a short status message.
* Show any loyalty change.
* Offer appropriate recovery actions.

The encounter should be short but memorable.

⸻

12. New encounter — Fleeing Pilot

Type: Recruitable pilot encounter

A lone pilot emerges from a damaged corridor, running from an unseen danger.

They have no usable mech.

The player may help, question, or leave them.

12.1 Primary reward

If the player successfully recruits the pilot:

* Gain one randomly generated Level 1 pilot.
* Roll Mental, Skill, and Endurance.
* Assign a random compatible specialization.
* Give three starting equipment slots.
* Begin with the fourth slot locked.
* Add the pilot to the persistent roster.

12.2 Madness chance

On recruitment:

80% chance: The pilot starts with 10–20% Madness.

20% chance: The pilot starts with 0% Madness.

Use the game’s normal 0–100 Madness percentage scale.

12.3 Madness recovery

The recruited pilot’s temporary Madness decreases by 2 percentage points per completed turn.

Examples:

* 10% → 8% → 6% → 4% → 2% → 0%.
* 20% → 18% → 16%, continuing until zero.

This means a pilot recruited at 10–20% can recover naturally over approximately 5–10 turns.

Do not reduce the pilot’s other Madness gains automatically unless they are part of the same temporary recovery pool.

Track the fleeing pilot’s initial recoverable Madness separately from later Madness effects so recovery cannot erase unrelated newly accumulated Madness.

Mental reduces the effects of their Madness while it remains active.

12.4 Encounter choices

Offer Shelter

* Recruit the pilot if they accept.
* Grant a safe transition to the roster.

Ask What Happened

* Reveal a short account of the danger.
* May provide a route clue or threat warning.

Provide Medical Help

* Spend a compatible resource.
* Improve immediate condition or recovery.
* May grant an initial cooperation benefit.

Send Them Away

* Decline recruitment.
* Pilot attempts to leave the area.

Recruit and Assign Immediately

* Available if a compatible unpiloted mech is present.
* The pilot enters active service with their remaining Madness.

12.5 Personality variation

New Level 1 pilots should have modest randomized character traits.

Examples:

* Nervous rookie.
* Overconfident trainee.
* Quiet maintenance operator.
* Wounded scout.
* Former recovery specialist.
* Frightened civilian-trained operator.

Do not imply that every fleeing pilot belongs to Relay Section R-8.

Some may be entirely unrelated to the pursuing mech team.

12.6 Event consequences

The fleeing pilot may have:

* Seen a dangerous machine.
* Escaped an earlier failed engagement.
* Lost a mech.
* Been separated from a friendly group.
* Witnessed an anomalous facility event.

These explanations can change initial dialogue and optional route clues.

They should not automatically establish major new canon events.

⸻

13. Integration with existing systems

Captive pilots

* Retain existing captivity states.
* Equipment does not automatically force cooperation.
* Loyalty barriers restrict assignments, not custody management.
* Existing capture limits remain intact.

Allied pilots

* Gain full equipment and leveling support.
* Can be assigned to compatible mechs.
* Can operate on foot where the game supports dismounted actions.
* May be injured, captured, or killed according to normal rules.

Relay Section R-8

* Existing named pilots receive appropriate starting stats and specialties.
* Captured R-8 pilots retain their identities.
* Loyal recruitment does not create duplicate pilots.
* Betrayal opportunities still use pilot personality and existing faction relationships.
* New equipment does not retroactively cancel established pursuit outcomes.

Improvised Army

* Builder Drones can repair compatible discovered mechs.
* Scrap Shepherds can recover eligible salvaged frames.
* Panic Jack can defend deployed allied pilots or relevant machines.
* Unfinished Warframes can interact with piloting only when explicitly compatible.

Cockpit equipment

Cockpit Parts come from eligible cockpit-equipped enemies.

Cockpit Wedge remains limited to blocking physical cockpit opening and ejection.

It does not block radio communications or protect a pilot from explosions.

18. Acceptance criteria

The expansion is ready for a playable test when:

1. Every captive and allied pilot has an individual equipment screen.
2. All pilots begin with three equipment slots.
3. Three uncommon Cockpit Parts can unlock a fourth equipment slot.
4. Captive and allied equipment categories work independently.
5. Pilots receive randomized Mental, Skill, and Endurance stats.
6. Levels provide progression and access to higher stat ranges.
7. Mental affects Madness and Nightmare resistance.
8. Skill affects accuracy and encounter choices.
9. Endurance affects ordinary damage resistance and survival.
10. Unwilling captives show orange-red assignment barriers.
11. Partially willing captives show generalized expectations.
12. Restricted captives have meaningful loyalty progression estimates or identified blockers.
13. All three new encounter types function.
14. Fleeing pilots are generated at Level 1 with the specified Madness behavior.
15. No equipment, pilots, resources, or encounter rewards duplicate across save/load or custody transfers.
16. Existing mech-team, capture, combat, and story systems remain functional.

19. OPEN / TUNE LATER

Do not silently establish:

* Final cockpit-part drop rate.

* Final stat ranges and caps.
* XP requirements.
* Higher-level specialization bonuses.
* Exact loyalty progression formulas.

Keep these configurable and separate from established canon.

Final design target

Pilots should feel like individual assets with their own strengths, equipment, risks, and development.

Capturing a skilled enemy pilot can provide a valuable new operator, but that pilot may remain dangerous or refuse cooperation.

Recruiting a frightened Level 1 survivor should feel different: they may initially be unreliable because of Madness, but could become a capable specialist through experience.

The fourth equipment slot gives cockpit salvage an additional purpose beyond chassis upgrades.

Most importantly, the player should have meaningful reasons to care about who a pilot is, not merely whether they are available to occupy a cockpit.

Each pilot should be someone the player can equip, develop, protect, negotiate with, or potentially lose.
