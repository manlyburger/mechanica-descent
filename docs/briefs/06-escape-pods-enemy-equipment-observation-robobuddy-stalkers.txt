MECHANICA DESCENT

Escape Pod Recovery, Enemy Equipment, Observation, Robobuddy Recovery & Threat Stalkers

Implementation Brief — Next Build

Status: PROPOSED implementation
Target: Claude / Mechanica Descent
Priority: Core combat improvements, persistent dungeon threats, and pilot progression

Requested additions

1. Post-combat opening of remaining escape pods, producing pilots eligible for their original mechs.
2. Equipment cards for deeper enemy mechs: Add-On Missile Pod, Extended Magazine, and Laser Sight.
3. Fourth pilot stat: Observation (Obv), allowing equipment to be discovered before its effects activate.
4. Enemy attacks distributed across random valid targets rather than disproportionately targeting Jubey.
5. Robobuddy reconstruction after combat using Scrap recovered from wreckage, preventing the associated Threat penalty.
6. High-level stalking enemies generated by Threat and scaled to current dungeon room progression.
7. Orange activity snippets at the top of branch-selection screens.
8. Defeating a stalking enemy reduces Threat by its level plus a difficulty-based bonus.

⸻

2. Post-combat escape pod opening

2.1 Core behavior

At the end of combat, any escape pods remaining in the completed encounter should be available for player interaction.

Do not automatically discard them when the final combat enemy is defeated.

Introduce a Post-Combat Recovery phase before final room departure.

This phase should identify:

* Remaining escape pods.
* Disabled or abandoned mechs.
* Recoverable wreckage.
* Capturable pilots.
* Ordinary salvage rewards.

Pod interaction

Each eligible escape pod displays:

OPEN ESCAPE POD

Selecting it initiates a pilot recovery event.

If the pod contains an unresolved occupant, roll or reveal a pilot appropriate to the mech from which the pod ejected.

Important continuity rule

If an escape pod already belongs to a known persistent pilot, such as a member of Relay Section R-8, preserve that exact pilot’s identity, level, stats, equipment, and story state.

Do not generate a second pilot.

Random pilot generation applies to anonymous or previously unidentified occupants, not established named pilots.

2.2 Pilot generation

When opening an anonymous occupied pod:

1. Retrieve the originating mech’s class and type.
2. Select a compatible pilot specialization.
3. Determine a valid level based on the originating enemy and dungeon depth.
4. Generate or retrieve the pilot’s Mental, Skill, Endurance, and Observation stats.
5. Assign a corresponding pilot equipment profile.
6. Generate condition and cooperation state.
7. Instantiate one persistent pilot record.
8. Present the encounter outcome.

Examples:

Originating mech	Eligible pilot
Needleback Courier	Recon / scout pilot
Latchback Brawler	Assault / melee pilot
Gantry Widow	Recovery / industrial pilot
Kilnheart Breaker	Heavy industrial / assault pilot
Relay Bishop	Command / electronic warfare pilot

Other cockpit-equipped mechs should use their own appropriate specialization tags.

Do not allow an escape pod’s pilot generation to create a duplicate reward each time it is opened.

2.3 Pod opening outcomes

An opened pod is not automatically a successful capture.

Possible outcomes:

Cooperative Pilot

* Occupant voluntarily exits.
* Player can discuss recruitment or custody.
* Generate appropriate cooperation state.

Frightened Pilot

* Attempts to flee or negotiate.
* May have temporary Madness.
* Can be secured if valid capture tools are available.

Hostile Pilot

* Resists opening or attempts to escape.
* May trigger a small post-combat confrontation.
* Can be subdued, captured, or allowed to flee.

Incapacitated Pilot

* Requires recovery or medical attention.
* Cannot immediately enter a new mech.

Empty or Previously Escaped Pod

* Only occurs if the actual pod state permits it.
* May contain minor salvage instead.
* Must not generate a new occupant.

Make hostile recovery events short and compatible with normal combat rules.

2.4 Player choices

For a recovered pilot, support:

* Attempt peaceful recruitment.
* Secure as captive.
* Offer medical assistance.
* Question or inspect.
* Release.
* Leave the pod unopened.

Equipment and custody restrictions still apply.

A pilot removed from the pod should be added to the correct roster or encounter state.

Capacity restrictions

Preserve:

* Clampjaw Drone: one captive.
* Mobile Containment Pod: one captive.
* Existing pilot-roster and custody capacity rules, if any.

If no secure holding capacity exists, offer valid alternatives rather than duplicating the captive or silently adding them to a full Pod.

2.5 Reward handling

Opening a pod may reveal compatible equipment or pilot belongings.

However:

* Each cockpit/pod has one eligible pilot roll.
* Each cockpit source has one Cockpit Parts drop opportunity where applicable.
* Opening a pod does not generate a second cockpit salvage roll.
* Named pilot equipment must remain associated with that pilot unless actually transferred.
* Defeated enemies cannot be repeatedly farmed through reopening their pods.

2.6 End-of-combat interface

Add a compact recovery section:

COMBAT COMPLETE — RECOVERY AVAILABLE

Example:

* 2 escape pods remaining.
* 3 wrecks available.
* 1 damaged mech chassis.
* Robobuddy recovery available.

Allow the player to inspect each object.

Do not force the player to open every pod before leaving.

Once the player leaves, unresolved objects follow normal room abandonment rules.

3. Enemy equipment cards

3.1 Core feature

Deeper enemy mechs can spawn with equipment cards that modify their combat performance.

Enemy equipment should be visually distinct from ordinary base attacks.

Each equipped enemy stores:

* Equipment identity.
* Equipment slot.
* Activation or passive effect.
* Remaining charges or ammunition.
* Whether the player has identified it.
* Whether the effect has already been revealed.
* Salvage eligibility.

Start with three equipment cards.

3.2 Depth scaling

Introduce increasingly frequent equipment as the player descends.

Suggested initial tuning:

Dungeon rooms reached	Equipment chance	Initial maximum
1–4	0%	None
5–8	25%	1
9–14	45%	1
15–20	65%	2
21+	80%	2

These are proposed values. Adapt to actual run length and encounter difficulty.

Bosses, authored pursuit enemies, and special encounters may have predefined equipment outside this normal roll.

Do not reroll equipment every time an enemy card is inspected.

Equipment is assigned once when the enemy instance is generated.

4. Add-On Missile Pod

Type: Enemy Weapon Equipment
Rarity: Uncommon
Role: Supplemental ranged attack

Appearance

A small external missile rack mounted on a mech’s shoulder, back, or side armor.

Use a clear missile-rack icon on the enemy card when identified.

Mechanical effect

Proposed:

* Grants one supplemental missile attack.
* Deals 2 damage to a valid target.
* Has a two-turn cooldown.
* Uses the normal targeting and accuracy systems.
* Does not grant a free full turn.

The missile attack may replace or augment an existing attack depending on the current action economy.

Prefer a clearly defined supplemental-weapon action that cannot fire repeatedly through unintended triggers.

Targeting

Choose from valid targets using the new enemy-targeting system.

The missile pod should be capable of attacking:

* Jubey.
* Allied pilots.
* Robobuddy.
* Builder Drones.
* Scrap Shepherds.
* Other valid allied combat entities.

Use existing range and cover restrictions.

Observation reveal

If detected before activation:

OBSERVED: ADD-ON MISSILE POD

Display its approximate capability and warn when it is preparing to fire.

If undetected, its first launch reveals the equipment.

Salvage

An intact missile pod may occasionally be recovered.

Do not automatically award a full usable copy after every kill.

Respect damage, salvage limits, and existing equipment acquisition rules.

⸻

5. Extended Magazine

Type: Enemy Weapon Equipment
Rarity: Common / Uncommon
Role: Sustained ranged fire

Mechanical effect

Increase a compatible weapon’s ammunition capacity.

Proposed initial bonus:

+50% magazine capacity, rounded up.

Example:

* Ordinary magazine: 4 rounds.
* Extended magazine: 6 rounds.

The enemy reloads less frequently and can sustain offensive pressure longer.

This equipment should not directly increase accuracy or damage.

AI behavior

An equipped enemy:

* Tracks additional ammunition.
* Reloads when appropriate.
* May continue firing during turns when an ordinary equivalent would be reloading.

Do not generate unlimited ammunition.

Observation reveal

OBSERVED: EXTENDED MAGAZINE

The player may inspect the equipment and see its increased ammunition capacity.

If undetected, the player can infer it after the enemy exceeds the ordinary weapon’s expected number of shots.

Special interactions

* Destroying a weapon subsystem may disable the magazine.
* Capturing the mech intact may improve recovery chances.
* A compatible salvaged magazine may become player equipment if the existing equipment framework supports it.

⸻

6. Laser Sight

Type: Enemy Accuracy Equipment
Rarity: Uncommon
Role: Precision targeting

Mechanical effect

Increase applicable weapon accuracy.

Proposed initial bonus:

+10 percentage points to accuracy.

Apply within the combat system’s existing final hit-chance limits.

Do not treat Laser Sight as guaranteed accuracy.

Target presentation

When an equipped enemy aims, show an appropriate laser or targeting indicator.

Before equipment identification, this may appear only during active aiming.

After identification, the player can inspect the equipment’s effect.

Observation reveal

OBSERVED: LASER SIGHT

The equipment becomes visible before its first attack when the Observation check succeeds.

The player can prepare:

* Defensive positioning.
* Cover.
* Target disruption.
* A preemptive attack.
* A compatible equipment-disable action.

Interactions

* Sensor damage may disable the Laser Sight.
* Smoke or ECM continues to affect targeting according to existing rules.
* The sight does not override invalid line of fire.

7. Fourth pilot stat — Observation (Obv)

7.1 Purpose

Add Observation, abbreviated Obv, to the existing pilot RPG attributes.

The full stat set becomes:

* Mental
* Skill
* Endurance
* Observation

Observation determines how often pilots detect enemy equipment or other specifically supported hidden tactical information before it becomes active.

7.2 Stat generation

Observation follows the same level-based randomization and progression rules as the other three attributes.

If the current stat generator uses different ranges from previous proposals, reuse its actual rules.

Existing pilots should receive one stable Observation value through a deterministic migration.

Do not reroll Mental, Skill, or Endurance while adding Observation.

7.3 Equipment discovery calculation

For an eligible enemy equipment instance, make a pre-activation Observation check.

Suggested base formula:

Discovery chance = 10% + (6% × effective Obv) − concealment penalty

Cap the result between 5% and 90%.

Example:

Observation	Chance before concealment
1	16%
3	28%
5	40%
8	58%
10	70%
12	82%

The actual formula should remain configurable.

7.4 Enemy concealment

Some equipment is easier to spot.

Suggested concealment modifiers:

* Add-On Missile Pod: 0.
* Extended Magazine: -10 percentage points.
* Laser Sight: -5 percentage points.
* Deliberately hidden special equipment: -15 or more.

These are initial values.

7.5 Party Observation

When multiple friendly pilots are available, use the highest eligible effective Observation value for the initial equipment-identification check.

This avoids excessive rolls from large allied teams.

Do not give every pilot a separate independent roll that makes detection nearly guaranteed.

A later specialized scouting action may permit another check, if that action has a real cost.

7.6 When to roll

Roll when:

1. An encounter begins.
2. An enemy enters the battlefield with previously concealed equipment.
3. A specific scouting action explicitly grants another opportunity.

Store the result per enemy equipment instance.

Opening and closing the interface must never reroll detection.

7.7 What detection reveals

Successful detection shows:

* Equipment identity.
* Basic gameplay effect.
* Relevant attack warning.
* Available counterplay.

It does not automatically reveal the enemy’s entire inventory or unrelated future actions.

An unidentified item becomes visible after it has produced an observable effect.

7.8 Other possible Observation uses

For future expansion, Observation could enable:

* Noticing suspicious cockpit damage.
* Identifying an escape pod’s condition.
* Detecting a disguised enemy weapon.
* Spotting branch hazards.
* Discovering suspicious prisoner behavior.

These are OPEN possibilities, not mandatory for this build.

8. Enemy targeting overhaul

8.1 Core objective

Ordinary enemies should no longer disproportionately focus on Jubey.

They should attack random eligible targets most of the time, reflecting the confusion and distributed threats of a larger machine battlefield.

8.2 Proposed targeting algorithm

For an enemy preparing an ordinary attack:

1. Build the list of valid targets.
2. Remove destroyed, unreachable, or otherwise invalid targets.
3. Respect weapon range, cover, elevation, and engagement rules.
4. Check any explicitly required scripted target behavior.
5. Choose random or tactical targeting.
6. Telegraph the selected action where applicable.
7. Execute the attack.

Suggested initial split:

75% — Random valid target

25% — Tactical target selection

Random targeting should be uniform among legal targets by default.

Tactical selection may consider:

* Low-HP targets.
* High-damage units.
* Vulnerable machinery.
* Enemy role.
* Melee engagement.
* Relay Bishop’s commands.
* Protected or marked targets.

8.3 Important exceptions

Preserve explicit tactical behaviors.

Examples:

Latchback Brawler

* May prioritize Bishop’s attacker when protecting the commander.

Gantry Widow

* May prefer recovery operations over attacks.

Relay Bishop

* May coordinate attacks against a designated target.

Kilnheart Breaker

* May prioritize nearby cover or heavy machinery when using a specific ability.

Special bosses

* May have authored attacks with prescribed targeting.

These should be actual behavior exceptions, not reasons to retain universal focus on Jubey.

8.4 Melee interaction

The new downward melee movement remains mechanically meaningful.

A melee enemy:

* Chooses a reachable target or approach destination.
* Moves downward toward engagement.
* Attacks only when its melee action becomes valid.
* May select a different target later if the original target dies or becomes unreachable.

Do not permit random target selection to bypass melee range.

8.5 Ally survivability

This change will expose machine allies and support characters to substantially more enemy attacks.

Rebalance where necessary, particularly:

* Robobuddy.
* Builder Drones.
* Scrap Shepherd.
* Panic Jack.
* Captive-operated mechs.
* Allied pilots outside their machines.

Avoid globally increasing every ally’s HP as a substitute for fixing targeting and recovery.

9. Robobuddy post-combat reconstruction

9.1 Objective

Because enemies now attack more random targets, Robobuddy is likely to be destroyed more often.

Make Robobuddy recoverable after combat using Scrap from an eligible wreck.

The successful reconstruction avoids the Threat penalty associated with losing Robobuddy.

9.2 Destruction state

When Robobuddy reaches zero HP:

* Change its state to Disabled / Wrecked.
* Remove it from active combat.
* Preserve its identity and upgrades.
* Generate or reference its recoverable wreck.
* Do not immediately delete its persistent character record.

Robobuddy cannot continue acting while wrecked.

9.3 Recovery at combat end

During Post-Combat Recovery, present:

RECONSTRUCT ROBOBUDDY

Requirements:

* Robobuddy is wrecked.
* At least one eligible salvage wreck exists.
* The wreck contains sufficient recoverable Scrap.
* The player has not already reconstructed Robobuddy for this destruction event.

Suggested initial cost:

2 Scrap taken from recovered wreckage.

This is a proposed value.

9.4 Recovery process

1. Select an eligible wreck with sufficient salvage.
2. Reserve the required Scrap.
3. Deduct it from that wreck’s available salvage.
4. Reconstruct Robobuddy.
5. Restore its active state.
6. Restore a defined amount of HP.
7. Preserve its existing upgrades and identity.
8. Clear the pending destruction penalty.
9. Record the recovery.

Suggested restored HP: 50% of maximum, rounded up.

9.5 Threat handling

Successful wreck-funded reconstruction prevents the normal Robobuddy-loss Threat penalty.

Important distinctions:

* Do not subtract unrelated existing Threat.
* Do not award an additional Threat reduction for repeated reconstruction.
* Do not apply the loss penalty first and later accidentally apply it twice.
* The reconstruction only cancels the pending loss consequence for that Robobuddy destruction.

If an existing Robobuddy-loss penalty already exists, reuse its actual value.

If none exists, introduce a configurable loss penalty only where required by the new mechanic rather than inventing unrelated global Threat increases.

9.6 If no salvage is available

Do not grant a free reconstruction.

If the game has a persistent downed-companion system, keep Robobuddy recoverable through a later eligible salvage opportunity.

Otherwise, retain the existing fallback recovery behavior and its applicable Threat consequences.

Do not silently fabricate Scrap.

The player must be able to understand why reconstruction is unavailable.

9.7 Salvage exclusivity

Scrap used for Robobuddy reconstruction cannot also be collected as ordinary currency.

If Scrap Shepherd or another machine is carrying the wreckage, resolve ownership using the existing salvage rules.

Do not allow multiple recovery systems to consume the same resource.

9.8 Robobuddy recovery UI

At combat completion:

ROBOBUDDY DISABLED

Eligible wreck salvage: 3 Scrap

Recovery requirement: 2 Scrap

Choices:

* Reconstruct Robobuddy.
* Collect salvage instead.
* Leave Robobuddy downed, if supported.

Display that successful reconstruction avoids the pending Threat penalty.

10. Threat-driven stalking enemies

10.1 Core concept

High Threat should cause powerful enemies to begin actively stalking the player through the dungeon.

These are not ordinary random reinforcements.

They are persistent threats with their own identity, level, encounter status, and movement activity.

Their strength should scale against the player’s current room count.

10.2 Threat threshold

Introduce configurable thresholds for spawning or activating stalkers.

Suggested starting bands:

Threat	Activity
0–2	Normal dungeon activity
3–4	Signs of dangerous enemies nearby
5–6	One active stalker may begin hunting
7–8	Stronger or more aggressive stalker behavior
9+	Additional pressure, subject to active-stalker limits

Adapt the numbers if existing Threat uses a different scale.

Do not replace its current established meaning.

10.3 Stalker generation

Each stalker receives:

* Stable stalker ID.
* Enemy archetype.
* Level.
* Current estimated region or route.
* Spawned-at room count.
* Current pursuit state.
* Whether the player has seen it.
* Whether the player has fought it.
* Whether it has been permanently defeated.
* Threat reduction value.
* Equipment loadout.

A stalker must not be repeatedly rerolled just because the player changes branch-selection screens.

10.4 Level scaling

High Threat should produce enemies above the current room’s normal danger.

Suggested rule:

Stalker level = expected enemy level for current room + Threat-based level bonus

Proposed level bonus:

* Moderate Threat: +1 level.
* High Threat: +2 levels.
* Extreme Threat: +3 levels.

If enemy levels currently follow a different progression formula, use the existing curve as the basis.

Record the stalker’s level when generated.

Do not change its level retroactively whenever Threat fluctuates.

10.5 Stalker archetypes

Initial possible types:

Heavy Hunter

* Direct, high-damage combatant.
* Advances along player routes.

Recon Stalker

* Tracks the player.
* Prefers interception and ambush.

Recovery Predator

* Searches for abandoned machinery.
* May disrupt salvage or rescue operations.

Nightmare Stalker

* Strong interaction with Madness.
* Uses unusual environmental warnings.

Use existing enemy families where possible.

Avoid requiring four completely new models for the first implementation.

11. Orange branch-point activity snippets

11.1 Core requirement

When a stalker is active, branch-selection screens should contain additional orange text snippets at the top, describing its activity.

These messages should feel like glimpses of a powerful enemy moving through the same facility.

They provide atmosphere and useful warnings without fully revealing the enemy’s location.

11.2 Example snippets

Early pursuit:

* “Heavy machinery grinds somewhere beyond the next junction.”
* “Orange warning lights flicker in a corridor you haven’t entered.”
* “Something large is searching the maintenance levels.”

Closer pursuit:

* “A damaged security door buckles inward somewhere ahead.”
* “The distant footsteps stop when you approach the junction.”
* “A hostile signal moves across the facility map.”

Imminent encounter:

* “A towering silhouette crosses the far end of the passage.”
* “The corridor shakes under heavy mechanical steps.”
* “Something is waiting beyond one of these doors.”

After defeat:

* “The heavy footsteps no longer echo through the lower levels.”
* “A hostile signal vanishes from the facility network.”
* “For the first time in several rooms, the machinery is quiet.”

11.3 Presentation rules

* Place the snippet above normal branch choices.
* Use orange text or a restrained orange accent.
* Keep it concise, preferably one or two sentences.
* Do not displace required navigation information.
* Update based on actual stalker state.
* Avoid repeating the same snippet every branch.
* Use a suitable accessible contrast ratio.

11.4 Mechanical connection

Snippets must correspond to real tracked activity.

Examples:

If the stalker is distant:

* General sound or sensor hints.

If approaching:

* Stronger nearby signs.

If waiting in a reachable branch:

* Specific local warnings.

If defeated:

* Appropriate aftermath message.

The text should not claim an enemy is immediately ahead when the stalker has actually moved elsewhere.

12. Stalker pursuit behavior

12.1 Pursuit states

Use:

Dormant

* Not yet actively pursuing.

Hunting

* Moving through the dungeon.

Approaching

* Close enough to influence branch warnings.

Intercepting

* Eligible to appear in a selected encounter.

Engaged

* Currently in combat.

Defeated

* Permanently removed from the active stalker pool.

12.2 Movement progression

Advance stalker activity when the player completes a room or selects a branch, according to the existing room-transition model.

Do not advance pursuit merely because the player opens a menu.

Stalkers can:

* Follow the player’s route.
* Attempt to intercept a future branch.
* Search nearby rooms.
* React to high Threat or combat noise.
* Appear during an eligible encounter.

Do not make their appearance entirely random without connection to their recorded pursuit state.

12.3 Encounter scheduling

The first implementation should allow one active stalking enemy at a time by default.

Additional stalkers at extreme Threat remain configurable.

An active stalker can appear as:

* A special encounter.
* An added high-level enemy in an eligible combat room.
* A branch interception.
* A pursuit event that leads to combat.

Avoid unavoidable overlapping boss encounters unless intentionally authored.

12.4 Relationship with Relay Section R-8

Generic Threat stalkers are not automatically members of Relay Section R-8.

Keep the two systems distinct:

Relay Section R-8

* Persistent authored pilots.
* Team Coordination.
* Replacement mechs.
* Named pursuit story.

Threat Stalkers

* General dungeon pressure.
* High-level enemy hunting.
* Room-count scaling.
* Threat reduction on defeat.

They may interact when an encounter explicitly permits it.

Do not accidentally spawn a captured or dead R-8 pilot as a generic stalker.

13. Threat reduction on stalker defeat

13.1 Core rule

When the player permanently defeats a stalking enemy, reduce Threat to represent eliminating an actual dangerous presence in the dungeon.

Requested formula:

Threat reduction = stalker level + difficulty bonus for the current room

Use the actual current room progression when resolving difficulty.

13.2 Difficulty bonus

Implement a configurable comparison between the stalker’s combat difficulty and the expected enemy difficulty at the current room.

Suggested initial calculation:

Difficulty bonus = max(0, stalker difficulty rating − expected room difficulty rating)

Convert the result into the same units used by Threat.

If these ratings use incompatible scales, normalize them before applying the comparison.

Example

Current room: 12

Expected enemy difficulty: 7

Stalker level: 9

Stalker difficulty rating: 10

Then:

* Stalker level contribution: 9.
* Difficulty bonus: 10 − 7 = 3.
* Total Threat reduction: 12.

This is an illustrative example, not a fixed balance value.

13.3 Threat bounds

Apply:

New Threat = max(0, current Threat − calculated reduction)

Do not allow negative Threat.

The reduction happens exactly once per unique stalker.

13.4 Defeat requirements

Only grant the full elimination reduction when the stalker has genuinely been neutralized as an ongoing dungeon threat.

Valid outcomes may include:

* Killed.
* Permanently disabled.
* Captured and securely removed from pursuit.
* Forced into a permanent withdrawal through an authored resolution.

Temporary stun, retreat, cockpit ejection, or chassis destruction while the pilot can return should not automatically count as permanent defeat.

For piloted stalkers, preserve ejection and replacement-mech continuity.

13.5 Combat feedback

At the end of the encounter, display:

STALKING THREAT ELIMINATED

Example:

* Enemy level: 9
* Room difficulty bonus: +3
* Threat reduced: 12

If Threat reaches zero, update subsequent branch snippets and ordinary Threat-dependent encounters accordingly.

Do not erase unrelated persistent enemies that are already physically present.

14. Integration of all new systems

14.1 Observation and stalkers

Observation can reveal a stalker’s equipment once a valid encounter begins.

Do not automatically reveal its entire route merely because a pilot has high Observation.

Branch activity snippets remain a separate information channel.

14.2 Robobuddy and random enemy targeting

Random targeting makes Robobuddy more exposed.

Wreck-funded reconstruction should give the player a way to recover Robobuddy without turning each death into a permanent punishment.

However, Robobuddy destruction still creates a short-term combat disadvantage.

14.3 Escape pods and Threat

A pilot recovered from an escape pod may be captured, recruited, released, or allowed to escape.

If an escaped pilot belongs to an existing pursuit team, preserve its actual reinforcement consequences.

Do not generate duplicate Threat from repeated pod interactions.

14.4 Enemy equipment and salvage

Enemy equipment should contribute to deeper-room danger.

Observation increases counterplay.

Careful capture or intact-mech recovery may improve salvage opportunities, but should not guarantee every installed item as a reward.

14.5 Threat stalkers and escape pods

A piloted stalker may have a cockpit and escape pod.

If that pilot successfully escapes, the stalking threat remains unresolved unless the game explicitly records a permanent withdrawal.

The player may pursue or capture the pilot.

Opening a remaining escape pod after combat should resolve the same persistent identity.

Final design target

This update should make the dungeon feel like a contested place with a persistent ecosystem of machines, pilots, wreckage, and hostile pursuers.

Players who build a large Improvised Army gain more tactical options, but enemies also target those machines.

Robobuddy becomes a recoverable battlefield companion rather than a disposable casualty.

Escape pods create new opportunities for recruitment and capture after combat.

Observation rewards attentive pilots by revealing dangerous enemy modifications before they activate.

Most importantly, Threat becomes more than a number.

High Threat means something powerful is actively searching for the player. Defeating that enemy should make the dungeon feel genuinely safer because a real danger has been eliminated.
