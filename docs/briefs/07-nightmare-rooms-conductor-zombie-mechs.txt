MECHANICA DESCENT

Nightmare Rooms — Escalation, The Captive Choir, Zombie Mechs & Persistent Horror

Detailed Implementation Brief for Claude

Status: PROPOSED — new implementation work, not confirmed gameplay.

Selected Nightmare Rooms:

1. The Captive Choir — expanded with a new Nightmare AI
2. The Hangar That Breathes — CASKET-13
3. The Cinder Reliquary — ASHMOUTH CHARGE
4. The Wrong Robobuddy
5. The Staircase with Too Many Landings
6. The Last Safe Room

Additional mechanics:

* Higher Threat thresholds accounting for per-room depletion
* Threat-influenced encounter choices and outcome probabilities
* Persistent Nightmare consequences
* Zombie mech appearances in later combat
* Glitching AI and radio transmissions
* Conditional interactions based on pilot stats, captives, equipment, and previous choices
* Stronger atmospheric presentation with meaningful counterplay

⸻

1. Core design goal

Red Hall encounters currently risk feeling like optional reward rooms.

Change this so entering one creates a meaningful Nightmare exposure, and selecting an option can introduce dangerous consequences beyond the current room.

Nightmare Rooms should provide unusually valuable discoveries but threaten the player through:

* Madness accumulation
* Hostile encounters
* Persistent enemy effects
* Suspicious machinery
* Pilots who are frightened, compromised, or difficult to trust
* Abnormal transmissions
* Delayed pursuit events
* Unstable powerful equipment
* False safety
* Distorted navigation

The player should be able to recognize dangerous possibilities before committing to them.

Not every Nightmare reward is a trap, and not every frightening event must be hostile.

Preserve agency, visible consequences, deterministic rolls, and the game’s existing state.

⸻

2. Revised Threat system

2.1 Account for Threat depletion

Important change: Threat now decreases as the player progresses through rooms.

Do not use the previous 3, 6, and 9 Threat thresholds for Nightmare escalation.

Those values were proposed before accounting for per-room depletion and would make severe consequences too frequent.

Use higher provisional bands.

Current Threat	Nightmare influence
0–9	Low: environmental abnormalities, isolated hazards
10–19	Elevated: deceptive machinery and stronger complications
20–34	High: persistent consequences and hostile recovery opportunities
35–49	Severe: stalker interference and major hostile encounters
50+	Extreme: dangerous combinations, catastrophic possibilities

TUNE LATER: Inspect the actual current Threat gain, depletion, maximum, and typical values during runs. If 50 is unreachable or 10 is routine, recalibrate these numbers rather than adopting an incompatible scale.

Use current Threat after the existing room-transition decay has resolved when generating the new room.

Do not apply another Threat decay just for entering or inspecting a Nightmare event.

2.2 Nightmare severity

Retain four Nightmare tiers:

Tier	Normal minimum room	Entry Madness
I — The Unquiet	1	+1
II — The Distortion	5	+2
III — The Malignance	10	+3
IV — The Rupture	15	+4

The room number sets the base tier.

Suggested escalation:

* Threat 20–34: +1 severity tier.
* Threat 35+: +2 severity tiers.
* Madness 60%+: +1 severity tier.
* Repeated consecutive Red Hall selection: +1 severity tier.

Cap the result at IV.

Do not allow multiple modifiers to produce nonexistent tiers or repeatedly apply exposure when reopening a room screen.

2.3 Option danger

Continue the previous Clean / Complication / Crisis model.

Suggested calculation:

Danger = clamp(Severity - 1 + ThreatBand + OptionRisk - Preparation, 0, 7)

For this calculation:

* ThreatBand = 0 at Threat 0–9
* ThreatBand = 1 at Threat 10–19
* ThreatBand = 2 at Threat 20–34
* ThreatBand = 3 at Threat 35+

The 50+ band unlocks additional extreme consequences rather than further increasing the numerical danger modifier beyond the cap.

Outcome probabilities:

Outcome	Formula
Clean	75% − 8% × Danger
Complication	20% + 3% × Danger
Crisis	5% + 5% × Danger

OptionRisk:

* Cautious: 0
* Standard: 1
* Reckless: 2

Successful preparation reduces Danger when supported by the particular encounter.

Store the selected event outcome when rolled. Reopening the interface must not reroll it.

2.4 Visible option previews

Each Nightmare choice should show:

* Resource cost
* Potential reward
* Clean probability
* Complication probability
* Crisis probability
* Possible delayed consequence
* Relevant stat/equipment advantages
* Whether an active stalker may become involved

Do not show a generic “dangerous” label while concealing known possibilities like an explosion or a Zombie mech appearing in the next combat.

Specific outcomes can remain mysterious, but the categories of danger should be visible.

⸻

3. Persistent Nightmare consequences

Introduce a small bounded system for Nightmare effects that can survive the originating room.

Suggested types:

Nightmare Pursuit

* A hostile entity may appear in a later eligible combat encounter.

Nightmare Transmission

* AI/radio dialogue can become corrupted temporarily.

Nightmare Contamination

* A particular mech, pilot, or item carries a condition until resolved.

Nightmare Instability

* A recovered machine or weapon requires stabilization.

Nightmare Echo

* A previously encountered room or enemy influences a future event.

These effects need unique source IDs, durations, resolution conditions, and explicit expiration rules.

They must not trigger repeatedly without limit.

Suggested maximum for the initial build:

* One pending Zombie mech encounter.
* One active transmission disturbance.
* One active temporary Nightmare navigation disturbance.
* Existing item-specific instability may continue independently.

Use the current run’s existing state management and seeded RNG.

4. Nightmare Room 1 — The Captive Choir

Availability: Severity I–IV
Themes: Captive pilots, artificial intelligence, false cooperation, communication, psychological horror

4.1 Room presentation

The player enters a dim containment ward.

Several pilots are secured inside individual compartments.

Their lips move together.

No sound comes from their mouths.

A single voice speaks through the ceiling.

The voice has perfect timing, yet its tone changes unnaturally between words.

Some captives look frightened. Others stare directly at Jubey without blinking.

A monitor above the compartments displays:

CHORAL SYNCHRONIZATION: ACTIVE

The voice introduces itself.

“Welcome, unexpected visitor. I have been practicing their voices.”

4.2 New Nightmare AI — THE CONDUCTOR

Introduce THE CONDUCTOR, a distinct facility-based Nightmare AI responsible for coordinating the captive ward.

It is not PRATTLE-9.

PRATTLE-9’s existing voice-only betrayal restriction remains unchanged. The Conductor is a separate anomalous system with its own authored capabilities.

Appearance

The Conductor manifests through:

* An enormous vertical wall monitor
* A collection of smaller screens
* Orange-red waveform displays
* A stylized face assembled from several asynchronous digital eyes
* An animated mouth made from overlapping audio waveforms

When the Conductor speaks, the other screens move slightly out of synchronization.

It should look controlled, calculating, and unnervingly pleased with its captive audience.

Personality

The Conductor behaves like an excessively polite instructor directing an unwilling performance.

It refers to the captives as its choir.

It takes offense when Jubey interrupts their synchronization.

It may offer useful information or a release arrangement, but its motives remain questionable.

Example:

CONDUCTOR: “Wonderful. An audience.”

CONDUCTOR: “They have been rehearsing for quite some time.”

CONDUCTOR: “Please don’t disturb the arrangement.”

CONDUCTOR: “One of them knows how to open the next supply room. Would you like to hear that part?”

4.3 Choir mechanics

The Conductor uses the facility’s sound equipment and anomalous signal system to influence how the captive pilots speak, react, and interpret instructions.

Do not assume the pilots are all loyal to it.

Different captive states:

Frightened

* Wants to escape.
* Can provide genuine information.
* May hesitate to cooperate while transmission effects are active.

Resistant

* Attempts to break synchronization.
* May reveal warning signs to Jubey.

Compromised

* Repeats the Conductor’s phrases.
* May provide misleading information.
* Requires verification before recruitment.

Hostile

* May genuinely assist the Conductor.
* Can attempt escape or sabotage when freed.

Incapacitated

* Cannot perform tasks normally.
* May require aid or recovery.

Use persistent pilots when the event references an existing named character.

Do not duplicate pilots already captured, killed, or assigned elsewhere.

4.4 Conditional ways to deal with the Conductor

The player can take several approaches.

A. Negotiate a Solo Performance

Available: Always

The player asks the Conductor to release one pilot in exchange for a specific service or concession.

Possible conditions:

* Spend 1 Energy to stabilize its failing equipment.
* Provide an eligible machine part.
* Allow it to finish one short diagnostic sequence.
* Agree to leave a particular terminal operational.

Clean: One eligible pilot is released and can be questioned or recruited according to their state.

Complication: Pilot is released but remains compromised or distressed.

Crisis: The Conductor exploits the interaction to trigger a stronger synchronization event, damaging trust or creating a later transmission disturbance.

The agreement should have real terms. Fulfilling or breaking them affects subsequent interaction.

B. Interrupt the Choir

Available: Always
Improved with: Skill, compatible sound equipment

Disable the ward’s local acoustic/signal system.

Possible requirements:

* Skill 6+ to identify the correct interface.
* 1 Energy for a controlled shutdown.
* Compatible maintenance equipment to bypass the ordinary access check.

Clean: Choir synchronization ends and captives regain ordinary communication behavior.

Complication: The system shuts down but damages another facility subsystem.

Crisis: A corrupted transmission is emitted before shutdown and becomes a pending Nightmare effect.

Do not create unavoidable damage simply for choosing a nonviolent technical solution.

C. Isolate the Conductor’s Signal

Available: Compatible relay or signal-control equipment

Use an actual applicable communications device to isolate the ward’s signal path.

Clean: Prevent further Conductor-origin transmissions from the isolated system.

Complication: Partial isolation; the player must disconnect another compatible component.

Crisis: A surviving channel carries a distorted transmission into the next eligible room.

The Silent Access Coupler should work only where its existing interface and channel restrictions permit it.

D. Question a Captive Individually

Available: A reachable compatible captive
Improved with: Skill, Observation, Mental

Ask a pilot what is happening.

A resistant or frightened captive may indicate that the Conductor is manipulating the group.

High Observation may reveal which pilot is responding independently rather than repeating synchronized behavior.

High Mental may protect the questioning pilot from an applicable Nightmare effect.

Clean: Reveal useful information and identify a trustworthy response.

Complication: Receive accurate but incomplete information.

Crisis: The Conductor mimics the captive’s voice or inserts a misleading answer.

The player should be able to discover the deception through clues or a valid follow-up action.

E. Open the Compartments

Available: Always, subject to actual locks

Attempt direct physical release.

Clean: Free compatible captives and resolve individual reactions.

Complication: A prisoner panics, resists, or requires additional control.

Crisis: Coordinated breakout, damaged restraint hardware, or an existing hostile recovery unit responding to the disturbance.

Do not automatically make all freed captives friendly.

F. Accept the Conductor’s Offer

Available: After its introduction

The Conductor offers valuable information or equipment if the player agrees not to interrupt the synchronization.

Possible reward:

* Rare pilot data
* A useful route
* Supply-room authorization
* A Nightmare card
* Information about a nearby stalker

The player can accept, negotiate, or refuse.

At high severity, accepting may cause a temporary transmission disturbance or a compromised captive condition.

5. The Conductor’s glitching transmissions

This is a major new horror presentation system.

After specific Conductor outcomes, nearby AI or radio transmissions may temporarily malfunction.

Examples

A normal pilot transmission begins:

ALLY PILOT: “Jubey, the corridor is clear.”

The text momentarily distorts.

TRANSMISSION: “Jubey, the corridor is cl—cl—”

Then an unfamiliar voice continues:

CONDUCTOR: “All clear. All clear. All clear.”

Another example:

RELAY SYSTEM: “No hostile contacts detected.”

The text freezes.

RELAY SYSTEM: “NO HOSTILE CONTACTS DETECTED.”

A second line appears:

UNKNOWN: “Then why is something moving behind you?”

Mechanical effects

A Nightmare Transmission may:

* Temporarily make one noncritical intelligence report unreliable.
* Trigger a clearly indicated signal-interference condition.
* Cause a false warning that the player can investigate.
* Reveal that the Conductor is still present in an eligible network.
* Interfere with an explicitly compatible communications-based encounter choice.

Do not scramble actual button labels or disguise the real player input controls.

Actual resource costs, combat actions, and consequential choices must remain readable.

The player must not lose a run because essential UI information was made unreadable.

Removal

Allow a disturbance to end through:

* A successful signal-isolation action.
* A compatible repair operation.
* A defined duration, suggested 1–3 room transitions.
* An authored encounter resolution.

A Conductor transmission should never permanently overwrite PRATTLE-9’s behavior or grant PRATTLE-9 forbidden digital betrayal abilities.

6. Nightmare Room 3 — The Hangar That Breathes

Availability: Severity III–IV
Primary reward: CASKET-13, the Mourning Engine

6.1 Horror presentation

The player enters a huge dark hangar.

The entire room seems to breathe.

A gigantic armored mech hangs in a repair harness.

Its orange reactor pulses slowly.

Every pulse makes the lights dim.

The cockpit appears empty.

When Jubey approaches, the machine’s head turns toward them.

A maintenance screen says:

PILOT: NONE

Another line appears beneath it.

SECOND OCCUPANT: UNRESOLVED

The screen then goes dark.

6.2 CASKET-13 — The Mourning Engine

Suggested initial properties:

* 14 HP
* 4 base damage
* Heavy chassis
* Powerful melee attack
* Nightmare splash attack every third eligible action
* +2 Madness on each deployment
* Unstable reactor requiring supervision or maintenance

CASKET-13 must feel significantly stronger than ordinary discovered mechs.

Its danger should come from specific authored systems rather than arbitrary betrayal.

6.3 Player choices

Power It Up

Spend 2 Scrap and 1 Energy.

* Clean: Activate the mech under a valid compatible control mode.
* Complication: Activate it with reactor instability.
* Crisis: Activate its hostile autonomous defense state.

Assign a Pilot

Use a compatible allied or captive pilot.

* Clean: Successful operation.
* Complication: Pilot suffers a temporary Nightmare synchronization effect.
* Crisis: Uncontrolled machine action or emergency extraction scenario.

A captive’s ordinary loyalty and betrayal mechanics still apply.

Inspect the Reactor

Requires Observation or Skill.

* Clean: Identify instability and improve later activation odds.
* Complication: Discover a necessary repair.
* Crisis: Trigger an abnormal reactor pulse and environmental hazard.

Strip Its Components

Recover rare mechanical parts without recruiting the mech.

* Clean: Valuable salvage.
* Complication: Partial salvage or equipment damage.
* Crisis: Defense systems activate.

Abandon the Hangar

Leave without the major reward.

The player retains the Nightmare entry exposure but avoids further deliberate risk.

6.4 New horror consequence — The Dead Frame

Under certain crisis outcomes, the hangar creates a persistent Zombie Mech encounter.

The machine may appear to have been deactivated.

Its reactor becomes quiet.

The lights go out.

The player leaves.

Then, in a later combat encounter, a damaged mech begins moving with no active pilot.

Its cockpit displays:

NO PILOT DETECTED

The machine continues advancing.

Zombie mech behavior

Proposed initial enemy: The Dead Frame

* 8 HP
* 2 melee damage
* Slow closing movement
* Abnormal damaged animation
* One telegraphed self-repair attempt
* No normal pilot ejection
* No ordinary pilot recruitment reward

Its movement should use the existing downward melee-closing system.

The frame can appear as a bonus enemy in the next eligible combat, but only if an event actually scheduled it.

Do not spawn it merely because the player saw spooky text.

6.5 Prevention

The player may avoid this consequence by:

* Successfully stabilizing the reactor.
* Dismantling the relevant anomalous control hardware.
* Fully resolving the hostile activation.
* Avoiding the activation action entirely.

If CASKET-13 itself becomes a player-owned mech, do not simultaneously spawn a duplicate of the same chassis as a Zombie.

Use separate explicit source identities if a later Zombie is a different abandoned frame.

7. Nightmare Room 4 — The Cinder Reliquary

Availability: Severity II–IV
Primary reward: ASHMOUTH CHARGE

7.1 Room presentation

A sealed weapons chamber contains a single huge explosive launcher.

Its casing is cracked.

Something inside the ammunition compartment moves when Jubey approaches.

A warning label flashes:

THERMAL STABILITY FAILURE

A second warning appears.

DO NOT ARM NEAR OCCUPIED COCKPITS

The lights flicker as the explosive’s temperature rises.

7.2 ASHMOUTH CHARGE

Initial proposed effects:

* One powerful explosive shot.
* 9 primary damage.
* 3 splash damage to nearby valid units, including friendlies.
* +2 Threat when fired.
* Starts with unstable Heat if not properly recovered.
* Maximum Heat 4.

The weapon should be extremely tempting but dangerous to carry.

7.3 Player choices

Take It Immediately

* Clean: Receive loaded weapon with manageable Heat.
* Complication: Weapon starts at increased Heat.
* Crisis: Containment failure creates an immediate emergency.

Stabilize Before Taking

Spend 1 Energy or satisfy an eligible Skill requirement.

* Clean: Lower starting Heat.
* Complication: Extra repair cost.
* Crisis: Temporary thermal hazard or loss of stabilization opportunity.

Strip Its Components

* Clean: Gain useful weapon salvage.
* Complication: Partial component recovery.
* Crisis: Unstable ammunition ignites.

Detonate in Place

Destroy the weapon remotely using a supported safe-distance procedure.

Possible benefits:

* Clear an eligible obstruction.
* Damage an existing nearby hostile system.

Consequences:

* No portable weapon reward.
* Explosion may increase Threat or alter an active stalker’s pursuit behavior if the noise is relevant.

7.4 Heat progression

Heat should increase based on actual applicable events:

* Carrying between room transitions.
* Firing the weapon.
* Exposure to relevant environmental heat.
* Certain Nightmare effects.

At Heat 3, display a major warning.

At Heat 4, the weapon becomes critically unstable.

Provide valid responses where physically possible:

* Vent.
* Repair.
* Fire at a suitable target.
* Abandon.
* Contain.

A catastrophic failure should occur only through an established, telegraphed condition.

7.5 High-Threat consequence

At Threat 35+, the weapon’s discharge may affect an existing high-level stalker’s pursuit state.

Possible consequences:

* The stalker moves closer.
* It learns the player’s approximate location.
* An eligible interception becomes more likely.

Do not invent another identical stalker.

The weapon itself remains valuable because it may be capable of destroying a dangerous enemy.

8. Nightmare Room 6 — The Wrong Robobuddy

Availability: Severity II–IV
Themes: Identity, imitation, companionship, mechanical horror

8.1 Room presentation

The player hears Robobuddy’s familiar mechanical sounds.

A small figure emerges from darkness.

It looks almost correct.

Its faceplate is a little too wide.

Its joints move a fraction too late.

The machine looks at Jubey, pauses, and makes a familiar greeting noise.

Then another identical sound comes from behind the player.

8.2 State-aware generation

The room must inspect the real Robobuddy’s current status.

Real Robobuddy active

The imitation appears separately.

The player can compare movement, sounds, and appearance.

Real Robobuddy wrecked

The imitation resembles a recovered version of Robobuddy.

The player must distinguish it from the actual repairable wreck.

Robobuddy unavailable

Present a clearly contextualized unusual robot encounter without falsely claiming that a real Robobuddy is nearby.

Never overwrite the real Robobuddy’s identity or upgrades.

8.3 Player choices

Approach the Imitation

* Clean: Reveal harmless salvage or a damaged machine.
* Complication: Imitation creates a temporary Madness effect.
* Crisis: It attacks or attempts to follow the player.

Send the Real Robobuddy

Requires active Robobuddy.

* Clean: Real Robobuddy detects the discrepancy.
* Complication: Temporary confusion or malfunction.
* Crisis: Imitation initiates a short confrontation.

Inspect with Observation

Higher Observation improves detection of:

* Incorrect joint movement.
* Improper sound timing.
* False identification.
* Unusual cockpit or control components.

Successful detection lowers the danger of later interaction.

Attempt Reconstruction

Only if the actual Robobuddy is wrecked and legitimate recovery requirements are satisfied.

Use real available wreck Scrap.

Do not allow the Nightmare event to conjure free Robobuddy upgrades or bypass existing reconstruction costs.

Destroy the Imitation

Resolve a proper combat encounter.

Potential salvage is bounded and cannot include duplicate permanent Robobuddy equipment.

Leave

Avoid a confrontation.

The player may retain an appropriate uncertainty or temporary Nightmare consequence if one was already legitimately created.

8.4 New delayed consequence — It Followed You

On a qualifying crisis, the imitation escapes the room.

It may return in the next eligible combat as a small hostile Nightmare unit.

Suggested properties:

* 4 HP
* 1 damage
* Can imitate one basic Robobuddy behavior
* No actual Robobuddy identity
* No permanent companion upgrades

An alternative high-severity version may instead schedule the Dead Frame Zombie archetype if justified by the encounter.

Only one pending Zombie encounter should be scheduled by default.

Do not endlessly stack imitation reinforcements.

8.5 Additional scary presentation

Possible effects:

* Two Robobuddy voices briefly overlap.
* The imitation copies a movement after an unnatural delay.
* Its eyes follow a target that hasn’t moved yet.
* A radio transmission identifies the imitation as the real machine.
* The real Robobuddy produces an alarm or distress reaction when near it.

Corrupted identification must be visibly questionable and cannot change the actual unit’s persistent ID.

9. Nightmare Room 8 — The Staircase with Too Many Landings

Availability: Severity I–IV
Themes: Impossible geography, false navigation, pursuit

9.1 Room presentation

The Red Hall opens onto a staircase descending through industrial darkness.

The player counts four landings.

A facility sign says:

SEVEN FLOORS REMAINING

After descending one flight, the sign now says:

SEVEN FLOORS REMAINING

Turning around reveals another staircase that wasn’t there before.

Far below, something mechanical begins climbing.

9.2 Player choices

Follow the Emergency Lights

Attempt to reach a legal connected exit.

* Clean: Arrive at a valid branch.
* Complication: Lose time or incur a minor hazard.
* Crisis: Emerge near an active hostile encounter or create a temporary pursuit consequence.

Map the Staircase

Use Skill or Observation.

* Clean: Reveal a safer route and reduce subsequent danger.
* Complication: Reveal partial mapping at additional cost.
* Crisis: Misleading navigation information requires a corrective action.

Follow the Voices

Investigate distant speech, radio static, or muffled calls.

* Clean: Find eligible salvage, a captive, or a useful route.
* Complication: Discover misleading signals or hazardous machinery.
* Crisis: Encounter an anomalous machine or activate a pending Zombie encounter.

Use a Pilot Scout

Send a cooperative unmeched pilot or suitable allied machine.

* Clean: Scout returns with route information.
* Complication: Scout becomes temporarily separated or distressed.
* Crisis: Scout encounters a hostile entity, creating an intervention opportunity.

Turn Back

Return to a legal route.

No refund of Nightmare entry Madness.

9.3 Severity escalation

Tier I: Primarily strange navigation.

Tier II: False voices and limited hazards.

Tier III: Hostile machinery, captive encounters, temporary displacement.

Tier IV: High-level enemy interception, Zombie approach, or significant navigation danger.

9.4 Zombie mech encounter

At high severity, the sound of approaching machinery may correspond to an actual Dead Frame.

The player can:

* Attempt escape.
* Prepare an ambush.
* Use an alternate staircase.
* Confront the Zombie.
* Attempt to obstruct its route.

If avoided, it may become the next eligible combat’s bonus enemy if its actual pursuit state supports that continuation.

Do not count a mere visual silhouette as a defeated enemy.

9.5 Navigation safety

The staircase must preserve:

* Actual room count.
* Legal branches.
* Save/load continuity.
* Encounter rewards.
* Active stalker identity.
* Real pilot locations.

Do not generate infinite rooms or recursively reroll the same staircase event.

10. Nightmare Room 10 — The Last Safe Room

Availability: Severity III–IV
Themes: False security, temptation, betrayal, survival

10.1 Room presentation

The Nightmare corridor opens into a clean, warmly lit maintenance room.

There are functioning repair machines, fresh supplies, and comfortable seats.

Everything looks remarkably normal.

A screen reads:

SAFE ROOM VERIFIED

Under it, a smaller line appears:

LAST INSPECTION: UNKNOWN

A nearby radio softly announces:

“There are no hostile contacts in this area.”

The message repeats.

Then it repeats in a different voice.

10.2 Core mechanic

The room may genuinely be safe, partially compromised, or actively dangerous.

Determine its condition when the room is generated.

Do not decide retroactively based on which choice the player selects.

Suggested room states:

* Genuine refuge.
* Equipment malfunction.
* Surveillance compromise.
* Active Nightmare trap.

The state affects available evidence and subsequent consequences.

10.3 Player choices

Rest

Recover an appropriate amount of condition or another supported resource.

* Clean: Genuine safe recovery.
* Complication: Temporary Madness or fatigue effect.
* Crisis: A hostile disturbance interrupts the rest.

Repair the Army

Spend Scrap or use eligible maintenance machinery.

* Clean: Repair compatible allied mechs.
* Complication: Additional repair cost or incomplete restoration.
* Crisis: Unexpected machine malfunction.

Inspect Security

Use Skill or Observation.

* Clean: Reveal the room’s actual safety condition.
* Complication: Reveal partial information.
* Crisis: Activate a dormant detection system or Nightmare response.

Gather Supplies

Collect bounded seeded loot.

* Clean: Gain supplies.
* Complication: Trigger a minor security or equipment issue.
* Crisis: Discover the supply system is being used as bait.

Seal the Room

Spend compatible resources to fortify or isolate the chamber.

* Clean: Reduce the chance of later interruption.
* Complication: Lock out some services.
* Crisis: Discover a serious hidden problem while securing it.

Leave Immediately

Avoid further interactions.

No refund of entry Madness.

10.4 Special horror consequence — The Safe Room Was Listening

If the room has a valid Nightmare transmission or surveillance state, it may be gathering information about the player.

Examples:

* A repair terminal displays a pilot’s name before the player enters it.
* The radio repeats a previously heard private conversation.
* A supply cabinet opens before being touched.
* A monitor displays the approaching silhouette of a mech not yet encountered.
* The exit light briefly shows an orange warning symbol.

These cues must correspond to a genuine authored state.

10.5 High-severity Zombie encounter

On an eligible Crisis outcome, the player may discover a damaged mech behind a maintenance partition.

It appears inactive.

The player leaves.

During the next eligible combat, the same damaged chassis appears as a Zombie mech.

This should be foreshadowed with:

* A distinctive chassis ID or visual silhouette
* A malfunctioning pilot-status light
* Unexplained mechanical movement
* A warning that the room is not completely secure

If the player destroys or otherwise neutralizes that chassis while still inside the safe room, cancel the pending encounter.

Do not spawn a Zombie from nowhere when the player already resolved its source.

11. New shared enemy — The Dead Frame

Type: Nightmare Zombie Mech
Role: Persistent delayed encounter consequence

Appearance

A damaged humanoid mech with:

* Broken armor.
* Orange-lit internal machinery.
* A destroyed or empty cockpit.
* Limp, irregular movement.
* Stiff limbs that suddenly jerk into position.
* Unnatural pauses between actions.
* An identification system that reports no pilot.

Its movements should feel unnerving without making its combat timing unreadable.

Proposed stats

* 8 HP
* 2 melee damage
* Slow approach
* One special repair action
* Nightmare classification
* No recruitable pilot by default

Combat actions

Lurch Forward

* Advances using the normal melee-closing system.

Dead-Hand Strike

* Performs a standard melee attack when engaged.

Reassemble

* Restores 2 HP after a visible preparation.
* Once per encounter.

Sudden Motion

* At high severity, may shorten its next approach delay if properly telegraphed.

Do not grant free attacks or unlimited healing.

Important continuity rules

* Zombie mechs have unique entity IDs.
* They cannot generate real pilots without an explicitly authored variant.
* Their cockpit parts, if salvageable, follow ordinary unique-drop rules.
* They do not duplicate the real Robobuddy.
* They do not duplicate an owned CASKET-13.
* Defeating one does not automatically grant a Threat-stalker elimination bounty.
* They are only counted as stalkers when explicitly registered as actual persistent stalking enemies.

Scheduled appearance

A qualifying Nightmare outcome may create one pending Zombie encounter.

Suggested placement:

Next eligible combat within 1–2 room transitions.

If no eligible combat occurs in that window, either place it in the next suitable encounter or expire it according to an explicit deterministic rule.

Do not interrupt mandatory story-only rooms or generate impossible placements.

12. Nightmare transmission disturbance system

Implement one shared distortion effect that can be triggered by:

* The Conductor.
* The Wrong Robobuddy.
* The Last Safe Room.
* An explicitly authored high-severity staircase event.

Visual effects

* Brief orange static.
* Duplicate speaker names.
* Small text interruptions.
* Overlapping transmission sound.
* Unnatural pauses.
* Incorrect identification warnings clearly marked as signal anomalies.

Possible gameplay consequences

* One temporarily unreliable route clue.
* A distorted ally report requiring verification.
* A contextual Observation or Skill check.
* An optional encounter to isolate the disturbance.
* A warning about a pending Nightmare entity.

Do not actually change previously selected player actions through UI corruption.

The game may depict broken equipment, but its controls must remain reliable.

Interaction with Observation

High Observation may reveal:

* Repeated voice patterns.
* Inconsistent machine IDs.
* False pilot-status messages.
* Duplicate transmission timestamps.
* Anomalous signal origins.

This creates another valuable use for Obv without making every mystery automatically solvable.

Interaction with Mental

Mental reduces applicable Madness penalties and provides its established Nightmare damage resistance.

It should not automatically reveal every deception.

Interaction with PRATTLE-9

If PRATTLE-9 appears during a transmission disturbance:

* It may notice the problem.
* It may become frightened or unusually quiet.
* It may comment on corrupted announcements.
* It may offer an eligible maintenance action.

Its own low-mood betrayals remain voice-only.

Do not grant PRATTLE-9 hacking, remote alarms, or digital attacks as part of the Nightmare disturbance.

⸻

13. Cross-room consequence rules

Every delayed Nightmare consequence must record:

* Originating room.
* Event choice.
* Source entity.
* Severity.
* Threat at generation.
* Pending consequence type.
* Intended activation conditions.
* Time window.
* Whether foreshadowed.
* Whether resolved or canceled.

Examples

CASKET-13 hostile activation

* May schedule The Dead Frame if a separate damaged chassis is awakened and escapes.

Wrong Robobuddy

* May schedule a hostile imitation or a justified Zombie variant.

Staircase

* May schedule or immediately resolve a pursuing Dead Frame.

Last Safe Room

* May schedule a hidden damaged mech for the next eligible combat.

Captive Choir

* May schedule a transmission disturbance or hostile retrieval consequence.

Never create the same consequence twice from one completed event.

If a high-severity event causes both an immediate encounter and a delayed effect, this combination must be explicitly authored and represented in its outcome preview.

14. Atmospheric direction

Nightmare Rooms should feel progressively more wrong.

Tier I — Unquiet

* Faint mechanical noise.
* Distorted shadows.
* Small visual inconsistencies.
* Captives who behave strangely.
* Machines reacting a little too late.

Tier II — Distortion

* Repeated radio messages.
* Machinery moving without operators.
* Unreliable records.
* Doors that seem unfamiliar.
* Slightly altered familiar characters.

Tier III — Malignance

* Distinct hostile presences.
* Abandoned machines preparing to move.
* Powerful but dangerous equipment.
* Transmission interference.
* Persistent consequences outside the room.

Tier IV — Rupture

* Major environmental abnormalities.
* Zombie mechs.
* Dangerous overlapping hazards.
* Severe power or containment failures.
* Possible stalker interference.
* High-value rewards worth substantial risk.

Keep horror grounded in the existing mechanical and facility aesthetic.

Use orange warnings, industrial sound design, abnormal machine movement, and malfunctioning interface elements rather than relying exclusively on jumpscares.

Final design target

The Red Hall should offer extraordinary opportunities, but crossing its threshold must feel consequential.

A room containing captive pilots might also contain a Nightmare AI that speaks in their voices.

A magnificent abandoned mech might be extraordinarily powerful—or leave behind something that follows the player.

An experimental explosive might save a desperate fight while bringing an existing stalker closer.

A familiar little companion might be an imitation.

An endless staircase might contain an actual machine ascending toward Jubey.

And a beautiful, clean maintenance room might be perfectly safe.

Or it might be the place where something learned the player’s name.

The key is that the player makes informed, meaningful choices—and the Nightmare Room remembers what happened.
